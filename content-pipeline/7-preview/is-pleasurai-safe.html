The flaw types matter more than the count. "Injectable chat" — a cross-site scripting hole — lets an attacker read your conversation in real time or hijack the session. Insecure file access leaves private data unprotected on the device. Hardcoded tokens publish the keys to a backend inside the app itself. None of these are exotic attacks. They are foundational protections, missed.

A regulatory gap sits underneath all of it, too.

Note. HIPAA only protects regulated healthcare entities — health plans, healthcare clearinghouses, and the providers and business associates that handle data on their behalf. AI companion apps aren't classified as healthcare products, so the intimate things you type into any of these apps — Pleasur.ai included — get none of that legal shield, even though people confide things to a companion they'd never say to a doctor. The app's own policy is the contract that actually binds it, which is why reading it matters.

The takeaway isn't to panic. It's that "safe" can't be something an app declares about itself. It comes down to which apps actually ship the protections — and which publish enough for you to check. What Pleasur.ai actually does with your data [GAIN]

Pleasur.ai's published privacy policy spells out what it collects, how it protects it, and the one line that matters most. The live policy states, word for word, "We do not sell your personal information." That's a written commitment, not a marketing claim — and where data monetization is a standing worry, it's the strongest single signal on the page.

Now the full scope, because you can't consent to a net you can't see. Pleasur.ai collects a wide range of data, which is normal for the category and worth knowing:

  • Account info — email, username, password, gender, display name, and profile details.
  • Usage data — your interactions with AI characters, chat messages, generated content, and preferences.
  • Device info — IP address, browser, operating system, device identifiers, and screen resolution.

That's a real amount of data, and informed consent only works when you can see what you're agreeing to.

Protection is where Pleasur.ai meets the standard so many apps in the study did not. The policy commits to encryption of data in transit (TLS/SSL) and at rest — both the wire and the server, not just one of the two. Payments run through PCI-DSS-compliant processors, the same standard your bank uses, and the platform works with vetted hosting and payment providers rather than handling everything itself.

Here is the limit you need beside all of that. Data is collected and retained, and deletion is not a clean wipe. Account data is kept while your account is active and for up to 3 years after deletion or your last activity for legal and business reasons. Financial records can be held up to 10 years to satisfy tax law. Marketing data persists until you withdraw consent or go inactive for two years. Standard practice for the category — but worth knowing before you sign up.

Frequently asked questions

Does Pleasur.ai store my conversations?
Yes. Chat messages and generated content are part of the usage data Pleasur.ai collects, encrypted in transit and at rest per its published privacy policy, with access and deletion rights attached. You can delete your account in Settings; some data is retained for a limited period after deletion for legal and business reasons.
Can anyone read my chats, or does Pleasur.ai train its AI on my conversations?
Here is exactly what the privacy policy does and does not say. It says your chats are collected as usage data and stored, encrypted in transit and at rest, and may be shared with the AI model providers that operate the service so they can generate responses. It says your data is not sold. What it does not publish is any separate statement promising your conversations are never used to improve or train models. Because that promise isn't on the page, a privacy-maximalist should treat chats as stored-and-processed, not ephemeral — written, retained, and passed to the model providers that run the service, not something that vanishes after you send it.
Has Pleasur.ai had a data breach?
As of June 2026, no data breach affecting Pleasur.ai has been publicly reported — it does not appear in breach-tracking databases (the Have I Been Pwned-style trackers) or in the security press coverage of the AI companion category. That's a statement about the public record, not a guarantee: it means no incident has been reported and left unrefuted, not that one can never happen. What Pleasur.ai's published security measures do show is encryption in transit and at rest — the protection many apps in the 2026 category study were missing.
What personal data does Pleasur.ai collect?
Account info (email, username, password, profile details), usage data (chats, generated content, preferences), and device info (IP, browser, OS, device identifiers). It states plainly that it does not sell your personal information, per its privacy policy.
Is Pleasur.ai GDPR compliant?
Pleasur.ai's published policy sets out GDPR, UK GDPR, and CCPA rights — access, rectification, erasure, restriction, portability, objection, and withdrawal of consent. Read the full policy and contact support to exercise them.
How does Pleasur.ai handle my data if I delete my account?

Account data may be retained up to 3 years after deletion for legal and business reasons, and financial records up to 10 years for tax law, per its privacy policy. So deletion isn't instant or total — it's a documented process with legally required holds.
