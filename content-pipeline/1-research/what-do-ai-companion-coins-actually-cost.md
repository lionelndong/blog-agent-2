## Deep web research findings

Deep research ran (Perplexity sonar-reasoning-pro; `-deep.md` saved). Key signals:

- **Coin metering is becoming the category norm**, and reviewers now treat *coin transparency* as a differentiator — one review explicitly translates 5,000 coins into "~500 images / 100 min calls." Verbatim framing: a reviewer calls a tier *"competitive on price but underlining that access is subscription-based, with coins acting as a metered layer on top."*
- **Pricing drift is real and confusing.** Older reviews describe pleasur.ai/companions as freemium with a "$19/month unlimited premium" plan and even "lifetime access"; 2026 reviews describe the three-tier coin model. *"Few sources explicitly note that the model has changed or reconcile these discrepancies, which can make it hard for readers to know which pricing description is current."* — a direct mandate to be the canonical, dated source.
- **The biggest coverage gap is the math:** *"Only [one] review attempts to translate a monthly coin allowance into a concrete number of images or call minutes"* — everyone else stays vague. Cost-per-action arithmetic is the white space.
- Secondary signal: payment-safety / "what shows on my statement" / cancellation anxiety is as salient to readers as the price itself — worth a short trust FAQ.

(3 surprising findings flagged for the return summary: market leader publishes no per-action spec; phantom "$19/mo" still circulates in live reviews; Candy.ai's annual price drops to $3.99/mo but with only 100 tokens — the cheap sticker hides per-token top-up costs.)
