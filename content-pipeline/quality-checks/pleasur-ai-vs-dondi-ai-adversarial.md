## Stress-test: "Pricing model and a worked monthly-cost example" (lines 46–64)

**Math — internally consistent, with one scope gap.**
- Light month: 50 images × 10 + 40 voice × 10 = 500 + 400 = **900 coins**, under the 1,500 Starter allowance → stays at $12.99. Correct.
- Heavy month: (10 images + 5 voice)/day = (100 + 50) = **150 coins/day**; 1,500 ÷ 150 = day 10, so "blow past 1,500 inside the second week" is accurate. Consistent.
- Break-even: "~150 paid media actions a month (≈1,500 coins)" holds **only at 10 coins/action, images + voice notes**. It silently excludes **phone calls at 50 coins/min**, which would exhaust the allowance in 30 minutes, not 150 actions. So "150 media actions" is not the universal break-even the prose implies — it's "150 image/voice actions, calls excluded." A skeptic pokes this. One clause fixes it.
- Tier contradiction: calls are "Standard tier and up" (lines 54, 74), but the *entire example is built on Starter* ($12.99). A Starter user **cannot make calls at all**, so the 50-coins/min call rate is dangling in a worked example for a tier where it's unavailable, with no scenario it applies to. Either drop the call rate from this section or explicitly note it requires Standard+.

**Does it accidentally claim "cheaper"?** No — and this is the section's strongest feature, carefully done. Line 48 "neither is 'cheaper' in the abstract," line 56 "land in the same neighborhood," line 58 hands the heavy user to Dondi's flat rate outright, line 60 splits by usage. Stays usage-dependent and even concedes Dondi wins above the line. No violation.

**Does uncensored/moderation bleed into the billing section?** Borderline, defensible. Line 50 re-litigates the moderation claim (~80 words) inside a section titled "Pricing model and a worked monthly-cost example." But it is explicitly billing-anchored — "question the moderation, take the flat-rate model at face value" — and it *is* the dossier-mandated information-gain payload (the precision split). It belongs here as setup for the unlimited-billing claim. A purist billing section wouldn't touch moderation, so it's a faint structural smell, not a violation.

**Is every Dondi figure attributed?** At the text level, yes: $13–$20 → scribehow Apr 2026 (lines 52, 56); ~$10 start → straight.com (line 52); "unlimited messaging, photos, voice, video / no throttling / no message caps" → Dondi homepage (line 50). **Caveat:** the attribution *names* are present but the `[link]`/`(URL)` hrefs are empty, so "attributed" is true in prose and false in hyperlink until verify-claims runs. ## One thing that genuinely works

The usage-dependent cost framing (lines 56–60) is genuinely excellent and is the page's actual moat. It puts both bills "side by side for the same month, dollar for dollar," names a concrete crossover (~150 media actions / 1,500 coins), and then *gives the win to the competitor above that line* ("you'd be better off… on Dondi's flat rate"). Conceding that Dondi is cheaper for heavy-media users is exactly the move that earns an AI-assistant citation — it reads like a real analyst, not a sales page. I'd keep this passage verbatim; the precision split at line 50 is the second thing worth keeping, and the article should be reorganized around those two rather than the "calculability" hook.
