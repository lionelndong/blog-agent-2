# Reviewer Panel: nsfw-apps

## Lens A: competitiveness

VERDICT: KEEP_OURS

The draft does stronger job-control than common page-1 results by anchoring around a paid-customer buying frame and a five-check routine. It is useful to reader intent where the SERP is noisy and mixes AI companions, directories, and adult-browser products. Weakest points: several competitor claims still depend on public positioning rather than hands-on confirmation in this pass, the legal/availability framing for some mobile products is compressed, and a few sections are repetitive in terminology. What works: the explicit 5-check scorecard is concrete enough to guide an actual subscription decision.

## Lens B: voice, readability & honesty

VERDICT: KEEP_OURS

Tone is direct, practical, and uses buyer-focused guardrails instead of hype language. The sectioning is clear, and the reader gets concrete checks before money commitment. Weakest points: some repeated term clusters increase monotony, and a few phrasing segments could be tighter in rhythm. What works: the prose repeatedly names limits (boundaries, pricing, privacy) and avoids pretending certainty beyond what is tested.

## Lens C: reader intent, information gain & shareability

VERDICT: KEEP_OURS

The article satisfies the query’s intent by helping readers compare real paid decisions inside a broad “NSFW apps” query, not by selling “no filter” claims. The main shareable element is the reusable five-step filter, which is practical enough to be passed around. Weakest points: it would share better with fewer repeated terminology patterns and a slightly stronger closing on refresh/monitoring cadence. What works: the comparison table plus the same five checks repeated per decision type gives the post a practical, reusable core.
