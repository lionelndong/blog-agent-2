## Answer first (3 sentences)

I keep this draft over both the Candy.ai /subscriptions page and the skywork.ai Candy AI Review 2025 — it is the only page in the set that publishes exact per-action numbers (10/10/50) and then does the arithmetic out loud, which is precisely the answer every other page hedges on. Candy's vendor page gives a sticker price and "100 tokens/month" but never says what a token buys, and skywork is a competent review whose every number is caveated "indicative, confirm in-app" because the vendor publishes nothing — so neither can hand a reader a number they can plan around. The draft wins on the one axis the whole SERP is starved for (knowable, citable, dated math), but it wins on substance while still leaving five soft spots an editor or competitor will poke at.

## The 5 weakest things (relative to what's ranking)

1. **Every citation is a literal `[link]` placeholder — the page's entire credibility currently rests on sources that are not attached.** There are ~15 bare `[link]` tokens: the BLUF tier prices (line 3), the coin-buy math (line 27), the Candy sub price (line 45), the top-up-pack range (line 59), the per-action estimate (line 71), and more. skywork.ai actually hyperlinks and dates its claims; this draft asserts "$9.99 to $299.99" packs and "2–5 tokens per image" with nothing behind them. Until `/verify-claims` runs, this is an *uncited* page making *harder numeric claims* than the pages it intends to beat — for a GEO/citation play, an uncited number is not a citable number, so this is the biggest single gap to #1.

2. **The Candy.ai subscription price is stated three different ways inside one article, and none matches the research fact-lock.** The lock (research line 85) says Candy is **$13.99/mo (1-mo), $8.99 (3-mo), $3.99/mo (12-mo)**. The draft says "about $12.99/mo month-to-month (advertised as low as ~$5.99/mo on an annual plan)" (line 45), then "Candy.ai's '$5.99/mo' headline ... the actual one-month rate is closer to $12.99" (line 57). So it quotes Candy's monthly as **$12.99 — which is Pleasur.ai's own Starter price** — and invents a "$5.99" annual the research never supports ($3.99 is locked). A reviewer with the Candy page open catches this instantly, and it directly undercuts the "we are the accurate, dated source" thesis the whole piece is built on. Fix to $13.99 / $8.99 / $3.99 everywhere.

3. **It never names Candy.ai in the body where the comparison actually happens — it goes coy exactly where the named-entity citation lives.** The BLUF name-drops "every 'how much does Candy AI cost' review" (line 5), but the comparison prose then hides the entity: "an opaque allowance" / "Candy.ai's published subscription" appears at line 45, yet the table (lines 73–80) and the metering section (66–82) talk about "opaque/estimated competitor" generically. An answer engine resolving "how much does Candy AI cost" wants the entity named with its number attached; softening it to a generic "competitor" forfeits the exact named-entity surface the dossier identified as the point. (Where it does name Candy, see weakness #2 — the number is wrong.)

4. **The competitor per-image estimate drifts — "2–5" then "2–4" — making the page look as loose as the reviews it criticizes.** Research locks reviewer estimates at "~2–4 tokens/image, ~3 tokens/call-min." The draft says "2–5 tokens per image" (line 45), "~2–4" in the metering prose (line 71), and "~2–5 tokens" in the table row (line 75). On the *competitor's* number, of all places, inconsistency is self-defeating: the page's whole pitch is precision. Pick 2–4 (per the lock) and use it in all three spots.

5. **Thin on the "lived monthly cost" demand, and the high-tier table column outruns what's validated.** The #4 result is a Reddit "cost breakdown after 3 weeks of testing" — the dossier flags that readers want a *lived* spend account, not synthetic arithmetic. The draft's profiles (lines 98–100) are clean but invented ("Light — 30 images, no calls: 300 coins"); there's no "here's what a real month ran." Separately, the at-a-glance table (lines 39–41) lists "~500 voice notes" (Standard) and "~1,000 voice notes" (Ultimate) where the research lock only ever validates the images/call-minutes columns at those tiers and the body text (line 27) silently omits voice notes — a body/table mismatch on the single most liftable asset on a GEO page, which an engine will quote verbatim.

## The 1 thing that genuinely works

**The "calculate your own monthly cost" section (lines 86–104) plus the transparent-vs-opaque wedge is the genuine, liftable information gain.** The three-step procedure ("estimate use → multiply by 10/10/50 → pick the cheapest covering tier") with a worked example (60 images + 20 voice + 15 min = 1,550 coins → Standard) is concrete, reproducible, and structurally impossible for any page-1 competitor to write, because none of them have published rates to multiply. Paired with the line that keeps it honest — "The win isn't that one platform is unmetered ... the win is that the rate is published, so the metering is something you can plan around instead of something that happens to you" (line 81) — this is the reason to keep the page.

So it frames the *estimated* numbers correctly but fumbles the *vendor-published* ones — the easier half to get right. Must fix before publish.

### Is the "hidden top-up-pack cost" angle present and credible?
**Yes — present and lands.** Line 59: *"the monthly allowance is a floor, not a ceiling: when it runs dry, you buy à-la-carte top-up packs ... roughly $9.99 to $299.99 a pop [link], so the real monthly cost can climb well past the headline subscription — and you only find out once the counter hits zero mid-month. Caveat: the $9.99–$299.99 range is reviewer-reported (research line 87) and must carry that attribution plus a real source once `[link]` is filled — not a bare assertion.

### Compliance — does it ever imply "no metering / no tokens / flat rate / unlimited everything"?
**No — clean.** The draft self-corrects toward the compliance rail repeatedly: "Pleasur.ai meters media by coins like everyone else" (line 69), "The win isn't that one platform is unmetered" (line 81). No "flat rate," no "no tokens," no "unlimited everything." Differentiator framed as transparency-of-metering throughout. Pass.
