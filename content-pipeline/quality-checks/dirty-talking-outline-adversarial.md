# Outline adversarial — dirty-talking

Skeptical read of `3-outlines/dirty-talking.md` against MECE coverage, BLUF on every opener, problem→agitate→solution arc, differentiation vs the SERP top-5, and whether visuals earn their place.

## Findings

- **[HIGH] The "examples" section is where this article lives or dies vs the SERP — it must be substantial, not a token table.** Competitors win on example volume (55/85/116 phrases). We deliberately go lighter to make room for psychology + recovery, which is the right call, but the examples section must still feel generous (a real spread per rung), or readers bounce back to the phrase-warehouses. The outline allocates ~360 words + a table + four H3s — adequate IF the draft fills each rung with multiple concrete lines. Watch this at draft.

- **[HIGH] Consent must be threaded, not siloed.** It appears in the "how to start" and "over text" and "recovery" sections — good — but it should never read as a one-time disclaimer. The current structure handles it well (consent in start, online-consent in text, consent-gated kink in recovery). Acceptable. Draft must keep it woven, not a boilerplate paragraph.

- **[MEDIUM] Product mention risk: the practice/AI-companion slot could read as a bolt-on ad.** One slot is designed in (the confidence/practice section), which is the correct, genuinely-relevant placement (private rehearsal). Guardrail for draft: frame strictly as practice that complements partner communication, never replaces it; one link only; don't repeat the product in the conclusion as a hard CTA.

- **[MEDIUM] MECE check is clean but "why it works" and "what dirty talk is" risk slight overlap on the normalization point.** The define section normalizes (it's common); the why section explains mechanism. Keep the define section on WHAT + normality, and the why section on MECHANISM only. Minor — draft should not repeat the Lehmiller "it's common" stat in both.

- **[LOW] Visual density is appropriate and SFW.** 4 visuals (1 concept illustration, 1 chart, 2 tables) across 7 sections, with `none` justified on the definitional/argumentative/practical sections. No people, ad-network safe. The intensity-ladder image + chart are slightly redundant (both show the ladder) — but they serve different jobs (intro mental-model anchor vs in-section self-selection tool) and sit far apart. Acceptable; if the image generates poorly, the chart can carry the ladder alone.

- **[LOW] Title is 47 chars, includes a keyword variant ("Talk Dirty"), and signals 18+ + beginner.** Good. Note the exact head keyword is "dirty talking"; the H1 uses "talk dirty" which is the natural-language synonym and the dominant how-to phrasing on the SERP. Acceptable for readability; the slug and meta will carry "dirty talking."

## One thing that works

The problem→agitate→solution intro ("every guide hands you 85 phrases and zero nerve to use them") nails the exact gap in the SERP and sets up the whole differentiated structure. The mild-to-bold ladder is a genuine organizing spine the competitors lack.

## Verdict: **PASS**

No CRITICAL findings. HIGH items are draft-stage watch-points (example generosity, woven consent), not structural defects. MECE holds, BLUFs are present on every H2, differentiation is real. Advance to product-mentions.
