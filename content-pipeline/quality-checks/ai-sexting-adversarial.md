# Adversarial read — ai-sexting (v2)

Read as a skeptical industry editor who has waded through 100 AI-generated "what is X" explainers for the adult-AI niche and is allergic to all of them. Brand: Pleasur.AI. Audience: 18+ adults, curious newcomers to experienced platform-comparers, ~8th–9th grade reading level. No rewrites below, just the weak points.

## The 5 weakest things

1. **The "is it cheating / sin / healthy" section hedges to the point of saying little.** Three sub-questions get folded into one section, and each gets the same move: "it depends on you." That's the honest answer, but the reader who searched "is ai sexting cheating" wants a sharper decision aid than "the hiding is usually the tell." One concrete heuristic carries it; the religious paragraph is softer filler that could be half the length.

2. **"The *what*. The *how* is where it gets interesting." plus "Decided it's right for you?" plus "Worth seeing side by side." are connective tissue that recurs at every section seam.** It's the explainer's tic — ending each section by teeing up the next. Used once it's a nice handoff; used at five seams it reads formulaic. A skeptic notices the rhythm before the content.

3. **The two cited claims are the only hard facts in 2,300 words, and one is soft.** "Tens of thousands of US searches a month" is fine. "Replika stripped out its erotic roleplay back in 2023" is the load-bearing factual anchor of the entire "why people go looking" argument, and it's stated as settled fact. If the citation doesn't nail the date and the scope, the paragraph's logic wobbles. Everything else is reasoning, not evidence — defensible for an explainer, but it leans hard on those two links.

4. **The comparison table is thin.** Four rows, and two of them ("Pricing", "Voice / image features") restate things the prose already said. A skeptic reading the table learns almost nothing the surrounding paragraphs didn't. It earns its place structurally (breaks up text, scannable) but not informationally.

5. **The privacy section asserts "some may use them to improve their models — your chats could become training data" without a single example or source.** It's the most concrete privacy claim in the piece and it floats. For a section whose whole job is "verify before you trust," modeling that verification with one named, checkable instance would be stronger than the abstract warning.

## One thing that genuinely works

The cannibalization discipline is excellent and rare. The piece consistently refuses to re-rank apps, routes purchase intent to the roundup once and cleanly, and keeps itself in the "concept/how-it-works" lane the way a real editor protecting a content map would. The product mentions (Companion Creator / Image Generation) are woven into the *explanation* of memory and persona rather than bolted on as ads — that's the right way to be product-led.

## Structural-issue count

Weak structural issues identified: **2** (section-seam formulaic transitions; thin comparison table). The cheating-section hedge and the two floating privacy/Replika claims are content/depth notes, not structural defects — the MECE section map itself is sound. Below the 3-issue threshold.
