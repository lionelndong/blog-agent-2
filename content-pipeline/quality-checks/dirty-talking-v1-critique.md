# Brutal v1 self-critique — dirty-talking (DEDICATION / D6)

Reading v1 as a hostile sex-and-relationships editor who has seen 200 of these. What's actually weak:

1. **The examples are too few and too tame to beat the SERP.** Three lines per rung. The #1–#8 results give 55, 85, 116. A reader who came for "what do I say" gets a thinner menu than every competitor. The differentiation (psychology + recovery) is real, but if the examples section underdelivers, the reader bounces before they reach the good part. WEAK. Fix: widen each rung to 4 lines and make them more vivid/specific, not generic ("you have no idea what you do to me" is a cliché). Add a short "make it yours" mechanic so the reader can generate infinite lines, which actually beats a finite list.

2. **"Why it works" asserts mechanisms it then half-supports.** "Arousal is mostly mental" is stated as fact with no anchor. The two cited studies are about communication/satisfaction and pillow talk — neither directly proves "the brain is the biggest sex organ." The section is good but slightly oversells the science. Fix: frame the mental-arousal point as the widely-held expert view, and let the two real studies carry the communication claim, which they genuinely support.

3. **The over-text section buries the single most useful tactic.** "Build a slow burn" is the whole game for beginners over text, and it gets one sentence. WEAK. Fix: give the slow-burn its own concrete mini-example (a 3-message escalation) so the reader sees the rhythm, not just hears about it.

4. **The intro's "with real research, not vibes" writes a check the body must cash visibly.** If the stats end up as quiet inline links, the "real research" promise feels thin. Fix: make sure the why-section reads as evidence-led, and the verify-claims links land on credible sources.

5. **The bottom line is fine but soft.** "You'll be surprised how fast the nerve catches up to the want" is a nice closer, but the recap doesn't restate the ladder, which is the one thing a reader should leave with. Fix: name the ladder in the recap.

6. **Minor: "training wheels" and "dip a toe / cannonball" are both swimming/cycling metaphors stacked close together.** Slightly mixed. Keep one.

Verdict on v1: solid 90, but the examples thinness (#1) and the buried slow-burn (#3) are real reasons a reader might prefer a competitor. v2 must widen examples, add a worked over-text escalation, tighten the science framing, and harden the recap. That's a full revision pass, not cosmetic.
