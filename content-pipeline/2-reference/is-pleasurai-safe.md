### Account deletion / data-deletion control (status: live — re-verified 2026-06-28)
- What it is: Users can delete their personal data at any time — verbatim from the live policy:
  "You may request deletion of your personal data at any time by contacting us **or deleting your
  account through your settings**." So there are two real exit paths: in-app (Settings) and support.
- Use-case for THIS article: the FAQ "How does Pleasur.ai handle my data if I delete my account?"
  and the "what Pleasur.ai actually does with your data" section. This is the single most concrete
  trust proof — a user-facing control they can verify themselves.
- How to demonstrate: describe the in-settings delete path in plain language. Pair it honestly with
  the retention caveat (account data may be kept **up to 3 years** post-deletion for legal/business
  reasons; financial records up to 10 yrs for tax law). Naming the limit is what makes it citable —
  don't imply deletion is instant/total. A `[VISUAL: screenshot]` of the account-settings delete
  control would be strong IF capturable (auth required); otherwise route to manual-capture.

### Privacy-policy-backed posture: no-sale + encryption + 18+ + GDPR/CCPA (status: live — re-verified 2026-06-28)
- What it is: live policy states verbatim "We do not sell your personal information"; "Encryption of
  data in transit (TLS/SSL) and at rest"; GDPR/UK GDPR/CCPA rights (access, rectification, erasure,
  restriction, portability, objection, consent withdrawal); "Pleasur.ai is not intended for anyone
  under 18 years of age."
- Use-case for THIS article: the BLUF direct answer + the data-practices section + FAQs ("Is
  Pleasur.ai GDPR compliant?", "What personal data does Pleasur.ai collect?").
- How to demonstrate: cite the live `/legal/privacy-policy` as the source of truth (it is a public,
  citable page — allowed in reader copy). LEAD with the no-sale commitment (strongest trust signal).
  Keep third-party-sharing copy generic ("vetted hosting and payment providers") — internal-stack
  scrub forbids naming vendors.
