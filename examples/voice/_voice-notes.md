# Voice notes — read alongside the voice anchors

The two anchors here are the brand's best work to date, promoted 2026-06-12. They're good anchors, not perfect ones — the board's standing critique of past output applies to them in milder form. When you imitate them, keep the good and consciously avoid the residue:

## Keep (this IS the voice)

- Second person, direct, evidence-led. The reader is a competent adult choosing tools.
- Plain words about adult topics: no euphemism, no porn-site hype. 18+, consent-forward, never sleazy.
- Concrete specifics: real prices, named apps, actual feature behavior ("tap the speaker icon next to a reply").
- Honest trade-offs — including where a competitor beats us. Recommending Nomi for emotional depth bought more credibility than any pitch.
- BLUF section openers; an opinionated thesis.

## Avoid (the residue the rebuild is killing)

- **The verdict-formula tic** — ending every section with the same "pick X for Y" sentence shape. Vary constructions.
- **Crutch words** — "honest/honestly", "plainly", "clear pricing" repeated as a chorus. Say it once, prove it elsewhere.
- **Uniform 2-sentence paragraph rhythm** — let paragraphs breathe; mix one-line punches with developed 4–6 sentence passages (see how the Zapier piece in `niche/` paces itself).
- **Thinness dressed as crispness** — a 40-word section summary is not a section. Specifics first, brevity second.
- **Naming internal tools** — never mention the stack (Strapi, Doppler, Semrush, etc.) in reader-facing prose.

## The bar

A reader lands here from Google with page-1 alternatives one tap away. The article wins by knowing more, showing more, and respecting their time — not by sounding "punchy".
