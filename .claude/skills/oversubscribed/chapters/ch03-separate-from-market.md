# Chapter 3 (Principle 2): Separate Yourself from the Market

## Core Idea
Don't compete inside an oversupplied industry — build *your own* market of loyal fans whose buying is linked to **your** market, not "the" market. You don't need everyone; you need "more people than you can handle."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Create your own market**: separate buyers one by one from the industry and make them part of something special. (Top actors, Moët & Chandon, Tory Burch, Berkshire Hathaway are all priced by *their* market, not the category.)
  - How: build a loyal tribe around your business, products, personality, and philosophy.
- **You Don't Need Everyone**: a tiny, dedicated base can make you oversubscribed. JP, a London trainer, went from £45/hr and burnout to **eight clients** paying up to £40,000/year (six months in advance), by repositioning to a market that wasn't "the London fitness market" — it was "the JP market."
- **Famous for a Few**: you only need to be famous to ~**5,000–10,000 people** (or 100,000–1m for a corporate). "Famous" = someone who's never met you but feels they know, like, and trust you.

## Key Concepts — The 5 C's of being "famous"
- **Consistent and repetitive message** — say what you do in one clear, credible way, repeatedly. "People need to hear something about 7–15 times before they 'get it'."
- **Content** — articles, blogs, books, reports, videos, podcasts, updates. Without it, "it's nearly impossible to become famous for even 500 people."
- **Commercial ecosystem** — products at several price points (£100, £1,000, much more) so people who know/like/trust you can buy.
- **Continuity** — a visible track record (so they can Google you) *plus* ongoing updates to follow.
- **Collaboration** — as you become known, structure what you say yes/no to with others who are famous to a similar group.

## Mental Models
- "Your income isn't linked to 'the' market; it's linked to *your* market."
- Better to be "famous for 5,000 people and stay that way" than chase "15 minutes of fame."

## Worked Example
**Bitcoin, 2013:** Most people hadn't heard of it and thought a maths-pegged digital currency was a joke. "It really didn't matter what 'most people' thought." A small number who "got it" bid the price from $20 to $1,000 in 12 months. The lesson isn't crypto — it's that demand from *your* market, not the mainstream's opinion, sets the price.

## Anti-patterns
- **Catering to "the market"**: it "will force your prices down and demand more and more from you until you snap." Get better at *finding* your market instead.
- **Linking your business to industry trends**: guarantees you "compete on price with everyone else."

## Key Takeaways
1. Build your own tribe; don't fight for share of a commoditized industry.
2. A few thousand true fans is enough for a wonderful, oversubscribed business.
3. Be "famous for a few" using the 5 C's: Consistent message, Content, Commercial ecosystem, Continuity, Collaboration.
4. Your market = people who highly value you, can afford you, and whom you understand deeply.
