# Chapter 8 (Principle 7): Nothing Beats Being Positively Remarkable

## Core Idea
"If people talk about you in a positive way, your marketing budget reduces to zero." In a world of reviews, social feeds, and search, the best long-term marketing is a product worth talking about plus a trusted personal brand.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Replace Your Marketing Budget with a Remarkable Budget**: traditional advertising can end up "marketing your competition" (you prompt the search; they win the sale). Move at least **50%** of your traditional marketing budget into being remarkable — investing in your product and customer service so customers do the marketing.
  - **Remarkable ≠ premium or gimmicks**: it means "being the best in your niche or micro-niche… genuine advantages, real benefits and a superior experience." You can be remarkably cheap, remarkably fair, or remarkable for the lengths you'll go to.
- **Build a Remarkably Trusted Personal Brand**: people connect with faces, voices, and words far more easily than logos. (Richard Branson "burying the hatchet"; Rob Gardner's Redington requiring every employee to blog monthly under their own name.)

## Key Concepts — why personal brands are powerful
1. **Brains are wired for people**, not symbols — "it's far easier to get people to recognize a face." People naturally recommend a trusted person.
2. **You're in the room when you're not in the room** — your brand acts on your behalf whenever people mention you, *if* they know your unique take.
3. **You keep it for life** — a strong personal brand outlives any one business and gives a head start into the next.

- Build personal brands with: videos, blogs, articles, books, photos, quotes, live appearances, talks, publicity, events.

## Mental Models
- The test of remarkable: "When someone asks 'Who should I buy from?' it is your name that should come up."
- "Gone are the days when a company… can survive as a faceless corporation." Companies that become oversubscribed leverage the personal brands of the people inside.

## Worked Example
**The DSLR ad that backfired:** An electronics company runs "Take Amazing Photos with Our New Digital SLR." You remember an upcoming holiday — but instead of going to their store, you Google "Digital SLR Camera," read reviews, find a cheaper online warehouse and a blogger recommending a *different* brand, and tweet for advice. "By now you have forgotten that the only reason you began thinking of buying a camera was because of the ad." The advertiser paid to get you thinking; "you will probably buy the product that is the most remarkable."

## Anti-patterns
- **Over-investing in advertising vs. the product**: in a connected world, ads can drive prospects to a more remarkable competitor.
- **Staying a faceless brand**: high performers don't want to be a faceless "asset," and buyers want to know the people behind the business.

## Key Takeaways
1. A remarkable product turns customers into your marketing team and drives cost toward zero.
2. Shift ~50% of marketing spend into being genuinely remarkable.
3. Remarkable means best-in-niche with real benefits — not gimmicks, not necessarily premium.
4. Build trusted personal brands for the people inside the business.
