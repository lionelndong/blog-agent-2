# Chapter 13 (Phase 5 of the CDE Method): Celebrate and Innovate

## Core Idea
Don't rush into the next campaign. The final phase captures **stories, numbers and insights** — then learns, shares, rewards, and rests — so you stop repeating mistakes and start compounding what works.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stories, Numbers and Insights**: after a campaign, "hunt down the stories, capture the numbers, interrogate the data — then learn, share, congratulate and reward those involved." Skipping this means repeated mistakes and lost assets.
- **Tell Your Stories**: people buy because of the story a product tells, not geography or age. (Bremont — a British watchmaker founded 2002, not Swiss, yet oversubscribed — sells on stories: two brothers, a father lost in a light aircraft, supersonic ejection-tested watches, red bezels for pilots who eject. "Before this company releases a watch they first start telling its story.")
- **So Many Great Hidden Stories**: stories need only be "authentic and human," not blockbuster. Every business is full of them — why it launched, the tough times, a rewarded employee, a friendship from a client relationship. Capture them in writing/video/audio and put them online.
- **The Truth is in the Results**: measure everything; numbers cut through the noise. "Even if we think we have a great idea but it bombs on the numbers, the truth is we didn't have such a great idea." (The upside-down newspaper ad whose response "fell off a cliff.")
- **The Debrief**: right after an event, gather everyone (staff, volunteers, directors, speakers, suppliers) in a circle and answer **five questions** while it's fresh.
- **Time to Party and Rest**: campaigns are exhausting; schedule a celebration *and* downtime, "regardless of the result."

## Key Concepts — The five Debrief questions
1. What would you score our team performance on a scale from 1 to 10, and why?
2. What do you think we did well?
3. What could we have done better?
4. What was a magic moment you noticed?
5. Anything else?

- **Measure relentlessly**: cost per lead, cost per sale, enquiries, unsubscribes, clicks, conversion %, brochure A vs B, long vs short sales conversations. Investigate *after* the campaign, not during.
- **Insights compound**: "The more insights you can take forward into the next campaign the faster you will grow."

## Mental Models
- "Most people who buy a Bremont watch aren't going to fly a B-2 stealth bomber… People simply love the romance, the history and the adventure." Sell the romance around a genuinely good product.
- "If you're not yet oversubscribed, you might not be telling enough stories."

## Worked Example
**The back-to-back burnout:** Priestley's team ran three massive events back-to-back over three months and made millions — "except the entire team was completely shattered." The week after, three important people resigned, denting morale and leaving a hole. The lesson: build in a party and scheduled downtime (no email, no calls) after every campaign. "I promise you that you'll be putting your business at risk if you and your team don't take some time off."

## Anti-patterns
- **Rolling straight into the next campaign**: "you'll repeat mistakes and have failed to retain valuable assets."
- **Taking your own story for granted**: founders get so close they "stop telling people."
- **Trusting opinion over data**: only the numbers reveal "the truth in the results."
- **Skipping rest**: burns out the team and triggers resignations.

## Key Takeaways
1. Close every campaign by capturing stories, numbers, and insights.
2. Tell stories — they're as much the product as the thing itself; find the hidden human ones.
3. Measure everything and let "the truth in the results" guide the next campaign.
4. Run a 5-question debrief; then celebrate and force scheduled downtime.
