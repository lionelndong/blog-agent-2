# Chapter 1: Introduction — What "Oversubscribed" Means

## Core Idea
A product or brand is **oversubscribed** when there are far more buyers than sellers — when demand massively outstrips supply and more people want something than capacity allows. The book is a recipe for engineering that state on purpose, for any quality business.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Oversubscribed (the definition)**: more buyers than sellers; demand exceeds your capacity to supply. Priestley's events sold *more* tickets than the 700-seat venue, with a waiting list forming.
  - When to use: as the single north-star condition for pricing power, profit, and not chasing clients.
  - How: deliberately keep capacity below demand rather than trying to serve everyone.
- **It's orchestrated, not accidental**: "This wasn't happening by accident. It was orchestrated to be like this." Being in demand is a designed outcome, not luck.

## Key Concepts
- **Demand massively outstrips supply** — the mechanical cause of every oversubscribed product.
- **Don't chase clients — curate them**: "You won't have to chase opportunities; you'll curate those that show up."
- **Quality-business precondition**: the methods are for businesses that genuinely serve people, not gimmicks or fast sales. "You must feel confident that your offering is something that genuinely serves people."

## Mental Models
- Think of your inbox as "a garden of prospects rather than an endless stream of tasks to follow up on."
- See change as a wave: most people get "swept out to sea"; you should be "paddling hard" to surf it.

## Worked Example
**The double-your-money buyback email (Sydney, Jan 2013):** Priestley emailed ticket-holders to a sold-out 700-seat event: "We have sold too many tickets… If you'd like to sell your ticket back to us… we will buy back your ticket today for DOUBLE what you paid for it." Context: most Sydney business events are free, draw under 100 people, and are run by locals — his was brand-new, premium-priced, with no staff on the ground. The buyback wasn't a gimmick; it was genuine proof the event was oversubscribed, and it signalled that fact to the whole market.

## Key Takeaways
1. Oversubscribed = more buyers than sellers. Everything in the book serves this one condition.
2. You don't need the whole market; you need more demand than your honest capacity.
3. The position is engineered through principles + a campaign method, not hoped for.
4. The methods only work on a product you're proud of and committed to long-term.
