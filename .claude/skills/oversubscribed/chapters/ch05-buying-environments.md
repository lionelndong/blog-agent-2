# Chapter 5 (Principle 4): Buying Environments Create Buyers

## Core Idea
"People don't buy for logic or reason; they buy because the conditions are right." Buying behaviour is stimulated by the **buying environment** — your job is to create environments where a crowd buys all at once, not to persuade people one at a time.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Transparency of movement**: let people see how many others want what you have, before they have to commit. (A Facebook post gauged interest at $795 for a 60-seat workshop; 175+ said yes within 48 hours; releasing only 120 tickets sold out in a day → ~$100k in the bank before flights were booked.)
- **People Don't Buy What Others Want to Sell. They Buy What Others Want to Buy.** The Galeries Lafayette Chanel queue vs. the street vendor shouting "half-price!" three streets away — near-identical bags, but people queue for the one others want to buy.
- **Turn Your Clients Into Celebrities**: celebrate the people already buying; make them the stars so others aspire to be like them. ("Take all that money you want to spend on getting a new client and spend it on celebrating your existing clients.")
- **People Don't Buy What They Need — They Buy What They Want**: "Need is logical and want is emotional. Emotions will win every time, even with the most intelligent people." (Singapore sells the most Ferraris per person despite 90km/h limits.)

## Key Concepts
- **The herd moves when the herd moves** — "Trying to get one member of the pack to run forward is hard unless they are part of a stampede." Cause a mad dash all at once.
- **Desperation repels**: "The more a potential buyer sees you are desperate to sell something, the more they wonder why you're so desperate."
- **Sell the want, package the need**: ask what people *want*, build/explain it that way, "then package up what they need along with it."

## Mental Models
- A Campaign Driven Enterprise engineers a "mad dash all at once" — never engages people "one at a time."
- Celebrate the buyer, not yourself: "Beat the drum for your clients" and let them pull a crowd.

## Worked Example
**The health business that flipped need → want:** A health business kept telling people what they *needed* to do; even when people agreed, they didn't buy — they bought the pill, the liposuction, the gimmick. The same business took off when it started talking about what people *wanted*. "The language changed, the conversation changed, and the energy towards buying something changed too. What didn't change was the actual product itself." (Charity: Water did the same — made giving desirable via "donate your birthday," raising $20m+/yr.)

## Anti-patterns
- **Selling one-at-a-time**: kills energy and momentum; the same database that fills a workshop in 10 days via transparency would take over a month sold individually.
- **Screaming that you want to sell**: stores everywhere, marked-down prices, "I'll do anything for a sale" — all turn buyers off.
- **Telling people what they need**: "You can go broke trying to tell people what they need."

## Key Takeaways
1. Design the buying environment; don't rely on persuasion.
2. Show transparency of movement — proof that others want in.
3. Celebrate existing clients so prospects aspire to join them.
4. Sell to the want (emotional), then bundle in the need (logical).
