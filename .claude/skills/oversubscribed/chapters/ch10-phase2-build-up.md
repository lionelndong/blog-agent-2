# Chapter 10 (Phase 2 of the CDE Method): Build Up to Being Oversubscribed

## Core Idea
Before you sell, build up demand by **signalling** your intentions and terms, asking the market to **signal interest back**, and then **educating and entertaining** those interested people until they're ready to buy. Don't ask for the sale — ask for the signal.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Power of Signalling**: tell people what's going to happen *before* it happens, explain your terms in advance, and get the market to signal intentions back before they act. (Glastonbury pre-registration, "buy blind," refund windows — 350,000 register for 120,000 tickets, sold in minutes.)
- **Naming Your Terms**: "If you want to work with us, you need to behave a certain way." State who you want, how many you can serve, and on what terms. "You'll never become oversubscribed if you're not willing to name your terms."
- **Don't Ask for the Sale — Ask for the Signal**: a sale is a binary choice (buy/don't), but "people aren't binary." A low-commitment signal (form, "like," reply) captures the 25–90%-interested people a binary ask would lose.
- **Transparency**: openly show how many you signalled and how many signalled back, so the imbalance is visible (Apple's PR signalling capacity and queues; a Facebook forum showing 5× interest per unit of capacity).
- **The 7-Hour Rule**: big purchase decisions take **about seven hours** of total thinking/contact. Don't try to close sooner; instead, provide ~7 hours of content, ideas, conversation and connection. (Reinforced by Google's **ZMOT** — ~**11 touches**.)
- **Products-For-Prospects**: scalable products given cheaply/freely to educate or entertain (a book, podcast, sample, workshop, report, party, tickets) that move people toward the **core offering** and ideally capture their details.

## Key Concepts
- **There's One of Me and Lots of You**: demonstrate one seller and many buyers. Transparency is "a double-edged sword" — it works for you with a crowd (70 buyers, one seller) and against you without one (one of 300 trade-show booths).
- **Think Mobile and Media First**: the first 1–5 interactions are on a phone, amid distraction. "No matter what your business is, you are also a media and technology business."
- **Educate and Entertain (the 80–20 rule)**: combine them but don't blend 50/50 — 80% education with light entertainment at the edges, or 80% entertainment with a little education sprinkled in. "Imagine if they stopped the Wimbledon final to give everyone a tennis lesson."
- **Brains Don't Know It's Digital**: blogs, videos, podcasts "count" toward the 7 hours like face-to-face time; celebrity endorsements work because audiences have already spent 7+ hours with the celebrity.

## Mental Models
- Goal of build-up: move interested people "from 'maybe I would' to 'I'd love to'."
- Keep 7 hours / 11 touches of relationship-building content available at all times (articles, podcasts, videos, apps, questionnaires, reports, books, case studies, events).
- Build the 7 hours in ways people *want* — parties, industry meetings, monthly coffees — "not in a creepy, annoying or pestering way."

## Worked Example
**The Australian masterclass (transparency of movement, again):** Instead of releasing tickets, the team posted on Facebook asking who'd attend a 60-person, $795 workshop, saying it would only run with 50+ interested per location. 175+ posted interest within 48 hours (many wanting to bring their teams). They then announced only 120 tickets, released at 9 a.m. the next day — almost all sold and paid by end of day one. The visible thread of interested people was the signal that made everyone act fast.

## Anti-patterns
- **Just putting a product up for sale and hoping**: what non-oversubscribed companies do; no signalling, no asked-for signal back.
- **Forcing a close before ~7 hours**: "you would be silly to try to force the deal to complete sooner than seven hours" for anything requiring trust or a significant decision.
- **Ignoring mobile/media**: "'We're not very techie' is the sound businesses make before they die" (echoed in Ch 12).

## Key Takeaways
1. Signal your intentions and terms first; ask the market to signal back.
2. Ask for the signal, not the sale — capture everyone who's even partly interested.
3. Make the imbalance visible through transparency (one seller, many buyers).
4. Earn ~7 hours / 11 touches via products-for-prospects that educate or entertain (80/20).
