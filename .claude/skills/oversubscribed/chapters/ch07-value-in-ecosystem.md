# Chapter 7 (Principle 6): Value is Created in the Ecosystem

## Core Idea
"Nothing works on its own; everything works together." You're no longer rewarded for delivering good value in isolation — you're rewarded for building a **product-and-services ecosystem** that works in harmony. "It's the ecosystem as a whole that creates the value, NOT any one product."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Value is created in the ecosystem**: don't measure any piece alone (Jamie Oliver's free TED talks, Twitter time, charity work, books, restaurants, sponsorships all subsidize and amplify each other). "You cannot expect to be highly rewarded for doing a good job… you can expect to be rewarded when you've built a high-performing ecosystem."
- **Give Away Ideas — Charge for Implementation**: information is now abundant and nearly worthless; *getting things done* is valuable. "Give away information freely or cheaply and then charge for the implementation work."
- **It's Easier to Climb Small Stairs Than to Jump Big Walls**: give a low-risk first step instead of demanding a big commitment. (Matthew Michalewicz raised $4m of *expressions of interest* after the dot-com crash by asking for non-binding indications, then firmed them up — vs. asking for money up front and getting "nos.")
- **Innovate — But Don't Mess with a Winning Formula**: innovate at the edges, protect the core (Porsche 911, LEGO bricks, Les Misérables, Vlados' "no change for 30 years"). Blackberry/RIM died trying to beat Apple at Apple's game (Alicia Keys tweeting from an iPhone).

## Key Concepts — The Spectrum of Value (low → high value to the client)
- **Information / ideas** — drowning in it; good to *give away* to show thought leadership. *(Example: a diet & fitness plan.)*
- **Components** — building blocks people assemble themselves; must sell a lot. *(A set of workout weights.)*
- **Supervision** — lowest form of implementation; close ongoing oversight while the client does the work; needs real accountability or it's just ideas. *(A gym.)*
- **Done with you** — moderate value; you and the client co-create the outcome. *(A personal trainer.)*
- **Done for you** — most valuable; a complete solution requiring little client time or energy. *(Liposuction.)*

**The less energy required from the client, the more valuable the product.** Weight-loss spectrum: $20 book → $500 gym → $3,000 personal training → $15,000 liposuction. Your business should offer products across these categories.

## Mental Models
- Your job is "to make a highly complex system seem effortless… turn chaos into order and do it with style."
- "Innovating out of a sense of boredom is dangerous. You might be bored, but the market may still be in love with what you do." (Foo Fighters must play "Everlong.")

## Worked Example
**The six-figure proposal that killed the sale:** A brilliant, 20-years-experienced consultant sent Priestley a plan with a six-figure estimate as a *first* deal. He agreed she was the best person for the job but couldn't commit such a sum cold — and "to this day, we've not moved forward." "Had she created a low-risk first step for us to climb we would have spent a fortune by now. Giving us a wall to climb killed the sale."

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to sell information**: "Don't go broke trying to sell information; make millions giving it away for free and then charging to make it happen."
- **Demanding a big first commitment**: "Making people jump over the wall freezes people into inaction."
- **Innovating the core out of boredom**: messing with a winning formula (RIM) can be fatal.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build an ecosystem; don't expect to be paid well for one good thing in isolation.
2. Give ideas away free; charge for implementation (Supervision → Done-with-you → Done-for-you).
3. Offer a low-risk first step — small stairs, not big walls.
4. Innovate at the edges; never mess with the formula that's winning.
