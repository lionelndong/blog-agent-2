# Chapter 14 (Part III): You, Your Team and the Crazy Times We Live In

## Core Idea
A wave of "convergent disruption" is breaking: functional work is losing value, "vital" connected people are gaining it. Choose the right game (Struggle / Lifestyle / Performance) and assemble a **CDE Team of four** — because "a surefire way to struggle is to try and do everything on your own."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Convergent disruption**: many technologies hitting at once (speech recognition, automation, robotics, data, collaborative sharing). "The value of 'functional people'… is sliding off a cliff," while "vital people" who organize, connect, and are well known rise in value. Every campaign, product, and partnership "is you paddling hard."
- **Struggle, Lifestyle or Performance (three games)**:
  - **Struggle** — clinging to the past, busy not productive, never gets oversubscribed.
  - **Lifestyle** — 3–4 enjoyable days/week, a team of 3–10, profitable, runs **2–4 campaigns/yr**, downtime between.
  - **High-performance** — 50+ hours, 50+ people, bold mission, big money; uses CDE to fill capacity fast then reinvests to *raise* capacity.
- **Lifestyle First, Then Performance**: "it's unlikely you'll get a high-performance business if you can't achieve a lifestyle one" (you can't summit Everest if you can't reach base camp). Get oversubscribed on a small capacity first; "your goal is to be oversubscribed, not necessarily to be *big*."
- **The CDE Team (four roles)**: Key Person of Influence; Head of Sales & Marketing; Head of Operations (& Product); Head of Finance, Logistics & Reporting. "Every company I've ever started began with a small team covering these four roles from day one."

## Key Concepts
- **Key Person of Influence — the 5 P's** (learnable strengths): **Pitching, Publishing, Productizing, Profile, Partnerships.** "Nothing really gets done without a Key Person of Influence."
- **Three layers of brand**: **Product** (iPod), **Company** (Apple), **Personality** (Steve Jobs). The personality brand is the most powerful today and where a small business can out-compete big ones.
- **Head of Sales & Marketing** lives for three things: **leads, sales, referrals.** Sales tools: **Script, Brochure, Diagram, Sign-up form, Term sheet.** Marketing assets: approved copy & images, approved content, lead-capture systems, measurement benchmarks.
- **Head of Operations** lives or dies by **Net Promoter Score (NPS)** — "How likely are you to recommend…?" Below ~7/10, no chance of profitable growth. Four levers: build a better product, build a better delivery system, change the target market, change the expectations.
- **Head of Finance & Reporting** unlocks cash: **fast reporting, better supplier terms, collections, access to finance/investment.** "A great finance person isn't ruining the party; they are making the party possible."

## Reference — The Seven Internal Maxims for High Performance
1. **You get what you pitch for and you're always pitching** — words have power; you "pitch into existence."
2. **Influence comes from output** — not coolness or manipulation, but prolific high-standard output.
3. **Income follows assets** — an asset is anything valuable tomorrow if the team got hit by a bus today (brochure, system, video, training doc, sales script).
4. **Get famous for the results of your clients** — let your results speak; showcase client success.
5. **You are in partnership with everyone who touches the business** — act from care for every party.
6. **Prolific beats perfect** — "Directionally accurate decisions are better than procrastination."
7. **Innovation never ends** — top companies spend **5–15% of revenue** on R&D, training, development.

## Mental Models
- **Functional vs. vital**: every day on functional tasks lowers your real value; every day building network/profile/influence raises it.
- **Recruit the team *first***: like running onto a football field alone to score before recruiting — "Clearly, this plan isn't going to work."

## Worked Example
**Priestley's founding team:** He started his first company at 22 with no track record — so he paid a retired "elder statesman" of the industry a percentage of every sale to be the **Key Person of Influence**; his name alone opened doors. His childhood friend **Glen Carlson** ran sales (50+ calls/day; 100 hot leads → 30+ sales), driving the business to seven figures in year one. **Marcus Ubl** ran operations, refining delivery and taking the business from ~$1m to ~$10m in sales. Four roles, from day one.

## Anti-patterns
- **Going it alone / "grow first then recruit"**: you get exhausted, fall behind, and lose to assembled teams.
- **Chasing performance before reaching lifestyle**: most failures jump straight to wanting "millions more in sales" without ever running a successful campaign.
- **Staying a faceless business**: no personality brand means starting from scratch after an exit.

## Key Takeaways
1. Functional work is devaluing; become a "vital," connected, remarkable person.
2. Win the Lifestyle game (oversubscribed on small capacity) before chasing Performance.
3. Build the four-role CDE Team from day one — never solo.
4. Lead it as a Key Person of Influence (the 5 P's) under the seven maxims; watch NPS and cash.
