# Chapter 9 (Phase 1 of the CDE Method): Campaign Planning — Know Your Capacity, Who It's For, and When

## Core Idea
Stop behaving like a "business" (busy-ness) and become a **Campaign Driven Enterprise (CDE)** — a series of well-timed campaigns. Phase 1 is planning: know your true capacity (how many people you can *delight*), who they are, and a 12-month rolling campaign schedule.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Campaign Driven Enterprise**: an identity, not a tactic. CDEs "look to cluster activities up into larger chunks" — get 100 prospects, choose 20 to work with, deliver, then get 100 more — instead of selling one at a time.
- **Capacity = how many you can delight**: not how many units you can ship. "The thing that matters when it comes to capacity is your ability to leave someone uplifted." (Guy Rigby limits new clients per quarter to protect quality.)
- **The Four Underlying Drivers of Value** (why people buy anything): (1) Save or make money; (2) Save time / eliminate wastage; (3) Bring increased emotional benefits; (4) Ease pain, suffering or negative emotions. *(All four explain why people buy a Rolex.)*
- **Who's Your Market? (the three-way test)**: a market that **highly values you, can afford to pay you, and that you want to serve**. "Even two out of three won't work." (David Boucher's $80k+ furniture; the yoga teacher with a broken formula — area can't pay, and she won't serve those who can.)
- **Clients versus Customers**: "A customer buys something. A client keeps coming back." A customer = a transaction; a client = a relationship. You need both.
- **Two products you must create**: a **product-for-prospects** (generates lots of happy customers at a low-risk price) and a **core offering** (a full, remarkable, evolving client relationship).

## Key Concepts
- **The Real Number**: your honest current capacity to create a *delighted* client (some answer "zero" — then the mission is to delight one).
- **Getting a Grip on Reality**: "Your goal isn't to tell people why they should be delighted; your goal is to watch and see if people ARE delighted." It's not the customer's job to fix it.
- **Create a Campaign Theme**: talk about something *bigger* than your products (Chipotle's "cultivating a better world"; Nike/Michael Jordan on failure; Apple "Think Different"). "What's impossible to compete with is when your campaigns talk about something bigger than your business."
- **Create a Campaign Timeline**: break each campaign into start/finish dates, communications schedule, milestones (price rises, cut-offs), supporting events, main events, shipping dates, and post-campaign activity — mapped on one visible timeline.

## Mental Models
- "A business that is humming along is often a series of well-timed campaigns" (like a car engine's revolutions).
- Plan a **12-month rolling schedule**: block holidays and training first, then sales targets, then monthly/quarterly campaigns. ("Training and development is key… spend 5–15% of your income on it.")
- Range: most businesses run **3–10 campaigns a year**; assume only ~10 productive months.

## Worked Example
**Breaking the capacity number into campaigns:**
- A consultant wants **12 clients/yr** at $30,000 (franchise/multi-location owners), with 3 good onboarding windows → **3 campaigns/yr, 4 clients each.**
- A hair salon needs **600 clients/yr** (busy young male professionals, ~4-week cut); has 350, needs 250 more → a campaign every **second month** for ~50 new clients.
- A startup wants **250 beta users** fast → a beta-launch campaign in month 1 and an expansion campaign in month 9.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pretending you have unlimited capacity**: "Once you try to exceed that capacity, your standards… have to drop" and you let down *all* clients, not just the extra ones.
- **Selling a commoditized product-for-prospects** (e.g. a £39 property ebook indistinguishable from free content): leads to refunds and resentment.
- **Talking only about your products** in campaigns: commoditizing and dull.

## Key Takeaways
1. Become a Campaign Driven Enterprise: work in waves, not one client at a time.
2. Capacity = how many people you can genuinely delight — name the real number.
3. Pick a market that values you, can pay you, and that you want to serve (all three).
4. Build a product-for-prospects *and* a core offering; plan 3–10 themed campaigns a year.
