# Chapter 4 (Principle 3): The Four Drivers for a Market Imbalance

## Core Idea
There are exactly **four** ways to create more buyers than sellers: **Innovation, Relationships, Convenience, Price.** Pick one as your main position — you can't dominate all four because each has an opposite.

## Frameworks Introduced — The Four Drivers
- **Innovation** — create something new and shiny no one else sells: one seller (you) + a niche of new buyers. *(Mamma Mia! repackaged ABBA into $2.5bn; the "Key Person of Influence" programme.)*
- **Relationship** — build such a strong bond that buyers ignore other sellers (your accountant; 24-month phone contracts; trusted influencers like Roger Federer).
- **Convenience** — answer the market with the most frictionless version of what they want; buyers won't invest time/energy finding alternatives (Amazon vs bookstores).
- **Price** — invest in an efficiency others lack so you can profitably undercut everyone (small lean services; Andrew Lloyd Webber buying London theatres).

## Key Concepts — the three sub-types of each driver
- **Innovation**: *Product* innovation (new/modified product — Star Wars), *Systems* innovation (deliver existing product faster/more reliably — Facebook), *Brand* innovation (make the boring desirable — Ralph Lauren's polo shirt).
- **Relationship**: *Become more influential* (enroll others in your ideas), *Become better known* (media, events, publicity), *Get deals in place* (contracts stop people shopping around).
- **Convenience**: *Better distribution*, *Better market information*, *Automation* (faster / on autopilot).
- **Price**: *Invest* (assets that are a barrier to entry — own premises/equipment), *Refine* (cut costs that don't add customer value), *Systemize* (use systems/tech instead of people).

## Mental Models
- **Each position has an opposite — so choose one main focus.** "You can't be highly innovative and convenient" (innovation takes time to scale); "you can't be super cheap and also compete on relationships" (relationships cost money).
- In big established markets you'll usually see four big players, each owning one driver, with a second as backup (hotels, airlines, banks, telcos, cars, computers).

## Reference Table — Drivers vs. how they create the imbalance
| Driver | Why buyers > sellers | Three levers |
|---|---|---|
| Innovation | Only one supplier of the new thing | Product / Systems / Brand |
| Relationship | Buyers ignore other sellers | Influence / Known / Deals |
| Convenience | Buyers won't hunt for alternatives | Distribution / Information / Automation |
| Price | Profitable price others can't match | Invest / Refine / Systemize |

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to win on all four**: they trade off against each other; you dilute your position and compete on price by default.
- **Relying on a trend (a convenience outcome)**: trends "come and go very quickly… anything that's trending is quickly accounted for with other sellers."

## Key Takeaways
1. More buyers than sellers comes from one of four drivers — name yours.
2. Choose one primary driver and (optionally) one secondary; the opposites can't be held together.
3. Each driver has three concrete levers to pull.
4. Convenience has the lowest barriers to entry today — fastest to copy.
