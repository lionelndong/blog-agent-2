# Chapter 11 (Phase 3 of the CDE Method): Release When Oversubscribed

## Core Idea
**Don't release your capacity until you are *sufficiently* oversubscribed.** Measure interest, confirm you've passed the threshold, then select your clients, manage the energy, have real sales conversations, and hold firm on price and capacity.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Oversubscribe Your Capacity — the three thresholds**: being *slightly* over (101 vs 100) isn't enough. You're sufficiently oversubscribed at:
  - **5× capacity in strongly signalled interest** — e.g. capacity 5, and 25 people paid a small holding deposit.
  - **10× capacity in educated/entertained prospects** — e.g. capacity 5, and 50 perfect clients attended your event.
  - **100× capacity in soft interest** — e.g. capacity 5, and 500 people downloaded your report.
  - Hitting *any one* gives confidence to release; if you've hit none, keep building up.
- **Measuring Interest**: track both **quantity** (downloads, registrations, deposits, opt-ins) and **quality** (weak vs strong commitment) on a single central **scoreboard**. Strongest = pre-payments/deposits; moderate = bought a product-for-prospects / detailed survey / attended an event; soft = a download, email opt-in, video view, competition entry.
- **Selection Process**: oversubscription lets you *choose* clients. Select the die-hard fans (who love what you do and create the hysteria — like Apple's store-queue fans); avoid the ones whose arm you twisted (buyer's remorse, complaints).
- **Managing Energy (three tools — plan them from the outset)**: keep buyers > sellers via **Staged Release**, **Special/Limited Editions**, and **Price Rises & Time Limits**. "It's critical not to use these tools on a whim mid-campaign… or it will come across as tacky."
- **Sales Conversations vs. Chit-Chats**: pre-sold ≠ ready to buy. A **sales conversation** is scripted, has brochures/materials, asks for the business, and ends in an order form / CRM notes; chit-chat does none of these. "Following up with sales conversations can quadruple the results."
- **Set Your Targets and Stay Firm**: don't raise price or expand capacity mid-campaign on a whim. "If you name a price, stick to that price."

## Key Concepts — the three Managing-Energy tools
- **Staged Release** — release capacity in smaller chunks/stages (Monty Python "one night only" → 9 more dates → 150,000+ tickets; Apple's "initial shipment").
- **Special Editions** — break capacity into smaller sub-groups (Star Wars box sets; Beatles re-releases; a version only for under-30s / a profession / high-net-worth).
- **Price Rises & Time Limits** — works *only* when it's a genuine deal for a genuinely short window (**under 24 hours**) that can't be found elsewhere. Fake "daily deals" train consumers to distrust you.

## Mental Models
- Three reasons to hold capacity firm: (1) saying yes when you can't deliver "sets you up to fail" — you risk letting down *every* client; (2) "people will learn not to take you at your word"; (3) "every person you turn away is a walking advertisement for your brand."
- "Being oversubscribed and running powerful campaigns doesn't remove the need to sell. It gives you more opportunities to sell and better qualified people to sell to."

## Worked Example
**The Monty Python staged release:** A "one night only" O2 reunion sold out in ~40 seconds; the evening news showed devastated fans. The next day John Cleese announced *nine more dates* (another 45,000 tickets) "due to outstanding demand" — re-igniting urgency ("tickets previously sold out in 45 seconds"). Over 150,000 tickets sold across staged batches. "Even when I know what they are doing it still works!" It's far more likely they used a planned staged release than genuinely couldn't book the venue.

## Anti-patterns
- **Releasing when only slightly oversubscribed**: not enough built-up energy; campaign loses momentum.
- **Raising price or capacity opportunistically mid-campaign**: erodes trust and your winning formula (Walmart/Sam Walton kept "stack it high, sell it cheap").
- **Refusing to have a sales conversation**: "because they hate asking for the business, they don't get much of it."
- **Fake scarcity / fake discounts**: "Consumers aren't dumb; they figure out pretty fast that it's not a 'deal' and it's not 'daily'."

## Key Takeaways
1. Release only after passing a threshold: 5× strong, 10× educated, or 100× soft interest.
2. Keep one scoreboard tracking both quantity and quality of signals.
3. Use staged release, special editions, and genuine <24h price/time limits — planned in advance.
4. Always run scripted sales conversations after the rush; hold price and capacity firm.
