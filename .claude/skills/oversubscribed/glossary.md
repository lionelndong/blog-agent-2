# Glossary — Oversubscribed (Daniel Priestley)

Terms in Priestley's own language. (Ch N) = where it's defined.

**80–20 (Educate/Entertain) Rule** — combine education and entertainment ~80/20, never 50/50; e.g. 80% education with light entertainment at the edges (Ch 10).

**Balanced** — demand ≈ supply; normal wages tolerated but not profit (Ch 2).

**Brand layers (Product / Company / Personality)** — three brands a business builds, e.g. iPod / Apple / Steve Jobs; personality brand is most powerful today (Ch 14).

**Campaign Driven Enterprise (CDE)** — the identity that turns a business into a series of well-timed campaigns; the engine for becoming oversubscribed (Ch 9).

**CDE Method** — the five phases: Planning, Build-up, Oversubscribed Release, Remarkable Delivery, Celebrate & Innovate (Ch 9–13).

**CDE Team** — the core four roles: Key Person of Influence, Head of Sales & Marketing, Head of Operations, Head of Finance & Reporting (Ch 14).

**Capacity** — how many people you can genuinely *delight* (not units shipped) (Ch 9).

**Chit-chat** — open-ended conversation with no script, materials, or ask; contrasted with a sales conversation (Ch 11).

**Client vs Customer** — a customer results from a transaction (buys once); a client results from a relationship (keeps coming back) (Ch 9).

**Convenience** — Driver 3: the most frictionless version of what the market wants; levers = distribution, market information, automation (Ch 4).

**Convergent disruption** — many technologies maturing at once, devaluing "functional" work and rewarding "vital," connected people (Ch 14).

**Core offering** — the full, remarkable, profitable client relationship you're known for; contrasted with the product-for-prospects (Ch 9).

**Done-for-you / Done-with-you / Supervision / Components / Information** — the spectrum of value; the less client energy required, the more valuable (Ch 7).

**Energy: Up / Down / Sideways** — emotional states after an experience; only *Up* (unexpected delight) gets people talking (Ch 12).

**Famous for a Few** — being known/liked/trusted by ~5,000–10,000 people (or 100k–1m for a corporate) (Ch 3).

**Five C's of being famous** — Consistent message, Content, Commercial ecosystem, Continuity, Collaboration (Ch 3).

**Four Drivers (of a market imbalance)** — Innovation, Relationships, Convenience, Price; choose one main position (Ch 4).

**Four Underlying Drivers of Value** — save/make money; save time/eliminate wastage; increase emotional benefits; ease pain/negative emotions (Ch 9).

**Innovation** — Driver 1: a new thing only you sell; sub-types = product, systems, brand innovation (Ch 4).

**Key Person of Influence (KPI)** — the known/liked/trusted leader who drives a project; strengths = Pitching, Publishing, Productizing, Profile, Partnerships (Ch 14).

**Managing Energy (tools)** — Staged Release, Special Editions, Price Rises & Time Limits; plan from the outset (Ch 11).

**Naming Your Terms** — openly stating who you'll work with, how many, and on what terms (Ch 10).

**Net Promoter Score (NPS)** — "How likely are you to recommend…?"; below ~7/10 means little chance of profitable growth (Ch 14).

**Oversubscribed** — far more buyers than sellers; demand massively outstrips your capacity to supply (Ch 1, 2).

**Philosophy** — your strongly held beliefs about what you do; polarizes the market and builds a tribe (Ch 6).

**Positively Remarkable** — "worthy of being talked about" for positive reasons; reduces marketing cost toward zero (Ch 8, 12).

**Price** — Driver 4: a profitable price others can't match; levers = invest, refine, systemize (Ch 4).

**Product-for-prospects** — a cheap/free, scalable product (book, podcast, sample, workshop, report, event) that educates/entertains and captures details (Ch 9, 10).

**Remarkable Audit** — scoring every touchpoint 1–10 on "Is this positively remarkable?" (Ch 12).

**Remarkable Budget** — money moved (≥50%) from advertising into making the product/service remarkable (Ch 8).

**Rule of 100 (note)** — *not from this book*; appears in Jonah Berger's *Contagious* — do not attribute to Priestley.

**Sales Conversation** — scripted, with brochures/materials, that asks for the business and ends in an order form / CRM notes (Ch 11).

**Seven-Hour Rule** — big decisions take ~7 hours of total contact/thought; provide ~7 hours of content (≈ Google's 11 ZMOT touches) (Ch 10).

**Seven Internal Maxims** — Priestley's culture maxims for high performance (Ch 14).

**Signalling** — telling people what will happen before it happens, and getting them to signal interest back before they act (Ch 10).

**Special Editions** — breaking capacity into smaller, limited sub-groups (Ch 11).

**Staged Release** — releasing capacity in smaller chunks/stages (Ch 11).

**Struggle / Lifestyle / Performance** — the three games entrepreneurs play; reach Lifestyle (oversubscribed on small capacity) before Performance (Ch 14).

**Story of the Two Bidders** — it only takes two people who want the one thing to drive the price up (Ch 2).

**Three states (Profits / Wages / Losses)** — Oversubscribed → profit; Balanced → wages; Undersubscribed → losses (Ch 2).

**Three oversubscription thresholds** — 5× strong signals, 10× educated/entertained, 100× soft interest (Ch 11).

**Transparency (of movement)** — openly showing how many people want what you have so the imbalance is visible (Ch 5, 10).

**Undersubscribed** — excess supply over demand; results in losses (Ch 2).

**70% Rule** — sell/promise only ~70% of what you'll deliver; keep magic in reserve for *Up* energy (Ch 12).
