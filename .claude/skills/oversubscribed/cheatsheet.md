# Cheatsheet — Oversubscribed (Daniel Priestley)

Decision rules and thresholds to act like Priestley would. Keep beside you while running a campaign.

## The One Rule
> **Demand must always exceed your capacity to supply.** You only profit when oversubscribed. Some willing buyers *must* miss out — that's the mechanism, not a bug.

## Three states → what you earn
| State | Demand vs Supply | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Oversubscribed** | demand > supply | **Profit** (on top of wages) |
| **Balanced** | demand ≈ supply | Wages only |
| **Undersubscribed** | supply > demand | **Losses** |

## Pick your driver (choose ONE main)
| If your edge is… | Driver | Pull these levers |
|---|---|---|
| Something new only you sell | **Innovation** | product / systems / brand |
| Buyers ignore other sellers | **Relationship** | influence / known / deals |
| Frictionless to buy | **Convenience** | distribution / info / automation |
| Profitable price others can't match | **Price** | invest / refine / systemize |
*You can't hold opposites (innovative+convenient, cheap+relationship). Choose one + one backup.*

## When is build-up "enough"? Release thresholds
> Release only when you pass **one** (capacity = C):
- **5 × C** strongly signalled (deposits, pre-payments) — or
- **10 × C** educated/entertained (attended your event, bought a product-for-prospects) — or
- **100 × C** soft interest (downloads, opt-ins, video views).
Hit none? Keep signalling and educating. Slightly over (C+1) is NOT enough.

## Signal-quality ladder (for the scoreboard)
| Strength | Examples | Multiple of capacity needed |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | deposit, pre-payment, finance pre-approval | 5× |
| Moderate | bought product-for-prospects, detailed survey, multi-hour event | 10× |
| Soft | download, email opt-in, video view, competition entry | 100× |

## Decision rules
- **Asking for commitment?** → Ask for the *signal*, not the sale. Offer a low-risk first step (small stairs, not big walls).
- **Selling something needing trust/learning/a big decision?** → Don't close before **~7 hours** of contact / **11 touches**. Have 7 hours of content ready.
- **Mixing education + entertainment?** → **80/20**, never 50/50.
- **Tempted to raise price or capacity mid-rush?** → Don't. Hold the named price and capacity (change only if pre-announced or after the campaign).
- **Running a price/time promo?** → Only if it's genuine *and* **< 24 hours** *and* unavailable elsewhere. Otherwise it backfires.
- **Designing delivery?** → Promise ~**70%** of what you'll deliver; surprise with the rest (aim for *Up* energy).
- **Starting a project?** → Recruit the **four-role team first**. Solo = guaranteed struggle.
- **Chasing "high performance"?** → First reach **Lifestyle** (oversubscribed on small capacity, 2–4 campaigns/yr). Base camp before Everest.

## Capacity = how many you can DELIGHT
Not units shipped. Energy types after an experience:
- **Up** (unexpected delight) → people talk → oversubscribed. ✅
- **Sideways** (satisfied, as expected) → silence → effort wasted.
- **Down** (disappointment) → negative word of mouth. ❌

## Tells & smells (you're off track if…)
- You're selling **one customer at a time** → no "stampede" energy. "The herd moves when the herd moves."
- You're talking about **what people *need*** → sell the *want*, bundle the need.
- You **can't say no** / won't name terms → you'll never become oversubscribed.
- You're **trying to sell information** → give it away, charge for implementation.
- Customers are merely **satisfied** → not remarkable; no word of mouth.
- You **skip the debrief / rest** → repeated mistakes, burnout, resignations.

## Campaign math (worked)
- Want **12 clients/yr** at $30k, 3 onboarding windows → **3 campaigns × 4 clients**.
- Run **3–10 campaigns/yr**; assume ~10 productive months; plan a 12-month rolling schedule (holidays + training first).
