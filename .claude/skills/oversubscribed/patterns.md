# Patterns & Techniques — Oversubscribed (Daniel Priestley)

Concrete, repeatable techniques from the book. Each: when to use, how, trade-offs.

## Make Your Own Market
**When**: you're competing on price inside an oversupplied industry.
**How**: build a loyal tribe around your philosophy; become "famous for a few" (5,000–10,000) via the 5 C's (Consistent message, Content, Commercial ecosystem, Continuity, Collaboration); link income to *your* market, not "the" market.
**Trade-offs**: requires sustained content output and a polarizing philosophy; you deliberately ignore most of the mass market.

## Choose One of the Four Drivers
**When**: positioning a business or product.
**How**: pick Innovation, Relationships, Convenience, or Price as your primary imbalance (plus an optional secondary); pull that driver's three levers.
**Trade-offs**: the drivers have opposites — chasing all four collapses you back to price competition.

## Transparency of Movement
**When**: launching anything to a warm audience.
**How**: gauge interest publicly (a Facebook post/forum, a registration list, a hashtag) before releasing; let people see that interest exceeds capacity; then release limited capacity.
**Trade-offs**: double-edged — a visible *lack* of demand works against you (one booth among 300).

## Don't Ask for the Sale — Ask for the Signal
**When**: any offer requiring trust or a non-trivial decision.
**How**: invite a low-commitment signal (expression-of-interest form, "like," reply, deposit) instead of a binary buy/don't ask; follow up with information and a sales conversation.
**Trade-offs**: adds a step and requires you to track and nurture signals on a scoreboard.

## Small Stairs, Not Big Walls
**When**: a prospect balks at a large first commitment.
**How**: offer a low-risk first step (non-binding interest, a cheap product-for-prospects, a trial), then firm it up once you're oversubscribed (Matthew Michalewicz raised $4m of indications post-crash).
**Trade-offs**: requires a designed entry product; pushing the big ask first "freezes people into inaction."

## The Seven-Hour / Eleven-Touch Build-Up
**When**: selling anything needing trust, learning, or emotional connection.
**How**: keep ~7 hours / 11 touches of content available (articles, videos, podcasts, books, events, questionnaires); educate/entertain at 80/20; never force a close before ~7 hours.
**Trade-offs**: costly to produce content; over-educating beyond the threshold can make you broke before you sell.

## Give Away Ideas — Charge for Implementation
**When**: your value is being commoditized by free information.
**How**: publish ideas freely to prove thought leadership; sell up the value spectrum (Supervision → Done-with-you → Done-for-you); the less client energy required, the higher the price.
**Trade-offs**: requires comfort giving away "secrets"; needs a real implementation offering behind the free content.

## Release Only When Sufficiently Oversubscribed
**When**: deciding to open sales.
**How**: confirm you've passed a threshold — 5× capacity in strong signals, OR 10× in educated/entertained prospects, OR 100× in soft interest — tracked on one scoreboard; otherwise keep building up.
**Trade-offs**: discipline to *not* sell early; measuring quality of signals takes judgment.

## Managing Energy: Staged Release / Special Editions / Price-&-Time Limits
**When**: releasing capacity while keeping buyers > sellers.
**How**: release in stages (Monty Python's added dates); create limited/special editions for sub-groups; run genuine <24h, can't-get-it-elsewhere price/time promotions. Plan all three from the outset.
**Trade-offs**: used ad hoc mid-campaign they read as "tacky and opportunistic"; fake scarcity/discounts destroy trust.

## Set Targets and Stay Firm
**When**: a rush tempts you to raise price or expand capacity.
**How**: hold the named price and capacity; only change if pre-announced or after the campaign ends.
**Trade-offs**: you forgo some quick sales — but keep trust, your winning formula, and word-of-mouth ("a walking advertisement").

## The Remarkable Audit
**When**: improving delivery / lifting word of mouth.
**How**: list every touchpoint (customers, team, suppliers), score 1–10 on "positively remarkable," improve 1–2 per month, invest 5–15% of revenue in the product/training.
**Trade-offs**: slow and uncomfortable; "the discomfort keeps the wannabes out."

## Under-Promise for Up Energy (70% Rule)
**When**: you want people to talk about you.
**How**: communicate ~70% of what you'll deliver; keep some good stuff secret so the surprise creates *Up* (delight) energy, not *Sideways* (satisfied).
**Trade-offs**: requires holding back impressive features at sale time; meeting expectations exactly produces no word of mouth.

## Tell Your Stories
**When**: a good product isn't standing out.
**How**: surface the human stories (why you started, tough times, client wins, hidden moments); capture in writing/video/audio; tell the story *before* releasing (Bremont watches).
**Trade-offs**: founders take their own story for granted; needs ongoing story-hunting.

## The Five-Question Debrief
**When**: immediately after any event/campaign day.
**How**: circle up everyone present; answer (1) score 1–10 and why, (2) what we did well, (3) what we could do better, (4) a magic moment, (5) anything else. Then party and schedule downtime.
**Trade-offs**: needs to happen *while fresh*; skipping rest after big campaigns causes burnout and resignations.

## Build the Four-Role CDE Team First
**When**: starting any new project or business.
**How**: assemble Key Person of Influence (the 5 P's), Head of Sales & Marketing (leads/sales/referrals + 5 sales tools), Head of Operations (owns NPS), Head of Finance & Reporting (unlocks cash) — from day one, under the seven maxims.
**Trade-offs**: harder than going solo up front, but solo guarantees exhaustion and lost business to assembled teams.
