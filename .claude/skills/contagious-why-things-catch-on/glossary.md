# Glossary — Contagious

**Arousal (physiological)** — A state of activation and readiness for action (racing heart, raised blood pressure); the driver of sharing. High arousal boosts transmission, low arousal suppresses it (Ch 3).

**Awe** — Wonder/amazement at something greater than oneself; the most-shared emotion in NYT science pieces (Ch 3).

**Behavioral residue** — Physical traces a behavior leaves behind that persist after use, providing lasting social proof (Livestrong wristbands, "I Voted" stickers) (Ch 4).

**Consider the context** — Designing a message so it's triggered by the audience's everyday environment, not just to be clever (Ch 2).

**Contagious** — Likely to spread; to diffuse person to person via word of mouth and social influence (Intro).

**Deactivating emotions** — Low-arousal emotions (sadness, contentment) that reduce sharing (Ch 3).

**Diminishing sensitivity** — The same absolute change feels smaller the farther it is from the reference point ($10 off a clock radio vs. a TV) (Ch 5).

**Exclusivity** — Limited *access* to something — by money, celebrity, or *knowledge*; makes people feel like insiders (Ch 1).

**Game mechanics** — Rules, feedback loops, and levels that quantify performance and enable status comparison (frequent-flier tiers, Foursquare badges) (Ch 1).

**Grow the habitat** — Create new links between a product and frequent environmental cues (Kit Kat ↔ coffee) (Ch 2).

**Habitat** — The set of triggers that cause people to think about a product or idea (Ch 2).

**High-arousal emotions** — Awe, excitement, amusement/humor, anger, anxiety, disgust — they "kindle the fire" and drive sharing (Ch 3).

**Immediate word of mouth** — Talk soon after an experience; driven by interest/novelty (Ch 2).

**Inner remarkability** — The latent novelty/surprise findable in any product, making it worthy of remark (Ch 1).

**Make the private public** — Generate visible signals for private choices/opinions (Movember moustaches) (Ch 4).

**Make the public private** — The inverse: hide how many others do an undesirable behavior to discourage it; stress the positive norm (Ch 4).

**Observability** — How easily others can see a product being used or a behavior performed; drives imitation and talk (Ch 4).

**Ongoing word of mouth** — Talk that continues for weeks/months; driven by triggers, not interest (Ch 2).

**Poison parasite** — Making a rival's message a trigger for your own (antismoking spoof of Marlboro) (Ch 2).

**Practical Value** — Useful information shared to help the receiver ("news you can use") (Ch 5).

**Prospect theory** — Kahneman & Tversky's account of how people judge value relative to reference points, not absolutes (Ch 5).

**Proof by analogy** — A story about someone like me makes me believe the same outcome applies to me; hard to argue with (Ch 6).

**Public** — The fourth STEPP: "Built to show, built to grow" — observable things spread (Ch 4).

**Reference point** — The expected comparison standard against which a price or value is judged (Ch 5).

**Remarkable** — Unusual, extraordinary, *worthy of remark/mention*; provides social currency (Ch 1).

**Rule of 100** — Frame discounts by price: percentage off under $100, dollars off over $100 (Ch 5).

**Scarcity** — Limited *amount* of something available; makes it seem more desirable (Ch 1).

**Social Currency** — The first STEPP: we share things that make us look good (Ch 1).

**Social epidemic** — Products, ideas, or behaviors diffusing through a population like a virus (Intro).

**Social proof** — Inferring correct behavior from what others do (lines, tip jars, sold-out items) (Ch 4).

**STEPPS** — The six principles of contagiousness: Social Currency, Triggers, Emotion, Public, Practical Value, Stories (Intro).

**Stories** — The sixth STEPP: narratives as Trojan-Horse vessels carrying ideas/brands (Ch 6).

**The 7% rule** — Only ~7% of word of mouth happens online; most is offline (Intro).

**Three Whys** — Heath brothers' technique: ask "why is this important?" three times to reach an idea's emotional core (Ch 3).

**Top of mind → tip of tongue** — The more accessible a thought, the more it's talked about (Ch 2).

**Triggers** — The second STEPP: environmental stimuli that prompt people to think (and talk) about related things (Ch 2).

**Trojan Horse** — A carrier narrative that smuggles your message inside a story people want to tell (Ch 6).

**Valuable virality** — Making the brand/benefit so integral to a story that people can't tell it without mentioning the brand (Ch 6).

**Word of mouth / social transmission** — Social influence and sharing; behind 20–50% of purchasing decisions and ~10× more effective than ads (Intro).
