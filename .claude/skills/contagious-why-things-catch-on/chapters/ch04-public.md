# Chapter 4: Public

## Core Idea
People imitate what they can see. Making products and behaviors more observable makes them easier to imitate and more likely to catch on. **If something is built to show, it's built to grow.**

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Public / Observability**: the easier it is to see others using a product or doing a behavior, the more it spreads (social proof + triggers + talk).
- **Make the private public**: generate public signals for private choices/opinions (Movember moustaches; "I Voted" stickers; Viagra coining "ED").
- **Design products that advertise themselves**: every use transmits passive approval (Hotmail signature; "Sent from my iPhone"; white iPod earbuds; Louboutin red soles; loud logos).
- **Behavioral residue**: the physical traces a behavior leaves behind that persist *after* use (Livestrong wristbands; reusable Lululemon bags; trophies; Facebook "Likes").
  - How: create durable, visible remnants that keep providing social proof when the product isn't in use.
- **Make the public private** (the inverse): to *stop* a behavior, hide how many others do it; highlight what people *should* do instead.

## Key Concepts
- **Social proof**: people infer correct behavior from others' behavior ("if there's a line, the food's good"; seeding the tip jar; 97.1% of kidney offers refused partly because others refused them).
- **"Behavior is public and thoughts are private"**: explains pluralistic ignorance — students binge-drink because they *see* drinking but can't see others' private discomfort.
- **Observability drives both imitation AND talk**: visible cars (LA/Miami) show stronger social influence than hidden ones (NY subway/Seattle rain); ~1 in 8 car sales attributed to social influence.

## Mental Models
- "**Monkey see, monkey do**" — but you can only copy what you can *see*; shirts spread, socks don't; cars influence, toothpaste doesn't.
- Ask: when someone uses my product, **does anyone else know?** If not, engineer a public signal or residue.
- For prevention, **don't advertise the prevalence of the bad behavior** — it normalizes it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Anti-behavior messages that broadcast prevalence**: "Just Say No"/National Youth Anti-Drug ads *increased* use by signaling "lots of peers do drugs." Music industry's "30 billion songs illegally downloaded" implies everyone pirates.
- **Petrified Forest sign** ("many visitors have removed wood") nearly *doubled* theft via social proof; the positive-framed sign worked.
- **Invisible value/behavior**: private giveaways (makeup cases) or hidden usage produce no social proof.

## Worked Example
**Livestrong wristbands.** Nike's Scott MacEachern weighed two ideas to honor Lance Armstrong and fund his foundation: a cross-country bike ride or a $1 wristband. Advisors called the band "a stupid idea." MacEachern's key choice: make it **yellow** — a striking, rarely-worn color (the Tour leader's jersey), visible from far away and gender-neutral. It became **behavioral residue**: unlike a one-time ride, "the wristband lives on… as a reminder every day." 5M sold in 6 months; 85M+ total. Public visibility manufactured a self-sustaining social epidemic.

**Movember.** Support for men's-health/cancer causes is normally private (you don't know who donated). Growing a moustache for November turns supporters into "walking, talking billboards" — it sparks conversation, transfers social currency, and recruits new participants. The foundation raised $174M+ worldwide. It works by **making the private public**.

## Key Takeaways
1. **Built to show, built to grow** — observability drives imitation, talk, and action.
2. **Make the private public**: create visible signals for hidden choices.
3. **Design self-advertising products** (signatures, distinctive colors/sounds/logos).
4. Create **behavioral residue** — durable traces that keep providing social proof.
5. To *prevent* behavior, **make the public private** and stress the positive norm.

## Connects To

- **Triggers (ch02)**: visible cues both prompt action and remind.
- **Practical Value (ch05)**: visible savings spread; hidden savings don't.
