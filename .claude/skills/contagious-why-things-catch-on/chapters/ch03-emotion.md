# Chapter 3: Emotion

## Core Idea
When we care, we share. But not all emotions drive sharing equally — what matters is **physiological arousal**. High-arousal emotions (awe, excitement, amusement, anger, anxiety) kindle the fire and boost transmission; low-arousal emotions (sadness, contentment) suppress it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Arousal (activation) framework**: classify emotions on TWO dimensions — positivity *and* arousal. Sharing tracks arousal, not just positivity.
  - **High-arousal → share more**: Awe, Excitement, Amusement/Humor (positive); Anger, Anxiety (negative).
  - **Low-arousal → share less**: Contentment (positive); Sadness (negative).
- **Focus on feelings, not features**: design messages around the underlying emotion that drives action, not facts/function.
- **The Three Whys** (from Heath brothers' *Made to Stick*): ask "why is this important?" three times to drill down to the emotional core of an idea.
  - How: state what people do → ask why ×3 → arrive at the deeper, emotional motive (search → find info → connect/achieve/fulfill dreams).
- **Make them mad, not sad**: on the negative side, choose high-arousal anger/anxiety/disgust over deactivating sadness.

## Key Concepts
- **Physiological arousal**: a state of activation and readiness for action (racing heart, raised blood pressure) — the fight-or-flight system; "kindles the fire."
- **Awe**: wonder/amazement at something greater than yourself; the most-shared emotion in NYT science articles (+30% to make Most E-Mailed).
- **Activation vs. deactivation**: high-arousal emotions drive *doing* (and sharing); low-arousal emotions make people power down.
- **Arousal from any source spills over**: even non-emotional arousal (exercise) boosts sharing.

## Mental Models
- Don't ask "is this emotion positive or negative?" Ask "**does it activate?**" Disgust (negative) shares; contentment (positive) doesn't.
- To boost sharing, **add arousal**: make a story angrier or an ad funnier and transmission rises.
- Catch people **when they're already fired up** — arousing TV/sites/contexts make adjacent content (ads) more shareable; arousal also explains "oversharing."

## Anti-patterns
- **Leading with information/statistics**: facts rarely move behavior; teens don't smoke because they lack data.
- **Defaulting to sadness or fear-without-action**: low-arousal sad appeals *reduce* sharing.
- **Avoiding all negative emotion**: marketers fear it, but high-arousal negatives (anger, anxiety, disgust) can powerfully drive talk — *if* on-message.
- **Ignoring high-arousal complaints**: angry/anxious negative WOM spreads (Motrin "babywearing" boycott); monitor for "pissed off/angry/mad" and intervene early.

## Worked Example
**The "running in place" experiment.** Berger had half of participants jog in place for 60 seconds and half sit quietly, then gave both a news article to optionally email. **75% of joggers shared it — more than twice the relaxed group** — even though running has nothing to do with the article. Proof: *any* arousal, even from a physical/situational source, boosts transmission.

**"United Breaks Guitars."** After United destroyed Dave Carroll's $3,500 guitar and stonewalled him for 9 months, he wrote an angry song and posted it. 1.3M views in 4 days; 3M+ in 10 days; United's stock fell ~10% (~$180M). High-arousal **anger** drove the share.

**Google "Parisian Love."** Search seems unemotional. Anthony Cafaro's Creative Lab told a love story entirely through evolving search queries ("study abroad Paris" → "translate tu es très mignon" → "how to assemble a crib"). By focusing on feelings it became a viral hit — "the best results don't show up in a search engine, they show up in people's lives."

## Key Takeaways
1. **When we care, we share** — but it's *arousal*, not mere positivity, that drives sharing.
2. Use **awe, excitement, humor, anger, anxiety, disgust**; avoid sadness and contentment as sharing drivers.
3. **Focus on feelings, not features**; use the **Three Whys** to find the emotional core.
4. **Make them mad, not sad** — and pair negative emotion with the on-brand message.
5. Find people (or moments) that are **already aroused**; arousal spills over to adjacent content.
