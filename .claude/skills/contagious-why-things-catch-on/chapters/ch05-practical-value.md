# Chapter 5: Practical Value

## Core Idea
People share useful information to help others — **news you can use**. Sharing practical value is a modern barn raising: it saves people time or money or improves their lives, and strengthens social bonds. To spread, useful content must *cut through the clutter* and be easy to package and pass on.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Practical Value**: helpful, useful information people share to benefit the *receiver* (vs. Social Currency, which is about the *sender*'s image). "Sharing is caring."
- **The psychology of deals (prospect theory — Kahneman & Tversky)**: deals are judged relative to expectations, not in absolutes.
  - **Reference points**: a price seems good/bad vs. an expected comparison price (the $350→$250 grill beats the $255→$240 grill, despite the higher final price). Infomercials and "regular price" tags set high reference points.
  - **Diminishing sensitivity**: the same $10 saving feels big on a $35 clock radio but trivial on a $650 TV — impact shrinks the farther from the reference point.
- **The Rule of 100**: choose discount framing by price.
  - Price **< $100** → frame as **percentage off** (20% off a $25 shirt > "$5 off").
  - Price **> $100** → frame as **dollars off** ($200 off a $2,000 laptop > "10% off").
- **Highlight incredible value + restrict availability**: scarcity/exclusivity (limited time, quantity limits "limit 3 per customer," restricted access) make deals seem better; quantity limits raised sales 50%+.

## Key Concepts
- **Reference point**: the expected comparison standard against which value is judged.
- **Diminishing sensitivity**: equal changes matter less farther from the reference point.
- **Packaging**: bundle expertise into short, scannable, list-style chunks people can easily pass on (Vanguard *MoneyWhys*; "Five ways to…").
- **Narrow audience paradox**: narrowly-relevant content can be *more* viral than broad content — it pops one specific person to mind, compelling a share (Ethiopian-restaurant article beats generic American one).

## Mental Models
- Sharing useful info is like **a barn raising / "pay it forward"** — a low-cost way to help and bond.
- A deal must be **remarkable** to be shared: if you shared every 10¢-off-soup, you'd have no friends; only standout value cuts through.
- **Make the value visible**: people can't share savings they can't see (hidden loyalty-card savings on a receipt vs. a checkout "you saved $X" signal).

## Anti-patterns
- **Always-on sales**: if everything's "on sale," the sale price becomes the new reference point and stops feeling like a deal; too many sale tags can *reduce* purchase.
- **Wrong discount frame**: using percentages on big-ticket items (or dollars on cheap ones) — violating the Rule of 100.
- **Spreading false "useful" info**: the drive to help spreads even fraudulent claims (the discredited vaccines-autism paper). Verify before passing on — false info spreads as fast as truth.
- **Assuming broad = shareable**: broad relevance doesn't trigger a *specific* share; narrow can win.

## Worked Example
**The barbecue grill (reference points).** Scenario A: a Weber grill marked down $350→$250. Scenario B: the same grill $255→$240. 75% would buy in A; only 22% in B — even though B's final price ($240) is *lower* than A's ($250). The higher reference point in A made its deal *feel* better. Takeaway: perceived value comes from the gap to the reference point, not the absolute price.

**Vanguard *MoneyWhys* (packaging useful value).** Vanguard sends a short, one-page monthly email — one header article plus 3–4 links — of genuinely useful financial tips. Berger forwards relevant ones to specific friends (homeowner's-insurance tip → friend who just bought a home). The practical value drives sharing, and Vanguard's expertise spreads along with it. Lesson: package knowledge tightly so it's easy to pass on.

## Key Takeaways
1. **News you can use** spreads — useful info helps the receiver and bonds people.
2. Value is **relative**: manage **reference points** and **diminishing sensitivity**.
3. Use the **Rule of 100** to frame discounts (% under $100, $ over $100).
4. **Restrict availability** (time/quantity/access) to make value feel bigger.
5. **Package** expertise into short, scannable units and **make savings visible**; verify before sharing.
