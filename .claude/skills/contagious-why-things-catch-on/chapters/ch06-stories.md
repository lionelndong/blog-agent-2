# Chapter 6: Stories

## Core Idea
People think in narratives, not information. Stories are **Trojan Horses** — vessels that carry ideas, lessons, and brand messages along under the guise of idle chatter. But virality alone isn't enough: the message must be *integral* to the story, or it gets left behind. Aim for **valuable virality**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stories as vessels (the Trojan Horse)**: wrap your product/idea inside a narrative people *want* to tell; "information travels under the guise of what seems like idle chatter."
- **Build your own Trojan Horse**: craft a carrier story that people share, talking about your product along the way.
  - How: find a remarkable/emotional narrative your audience already wants to discuss, then embed your message in it.
- **Valuable virality**: make the brand/benefit so woven into the plot that people **can't tell the story without it**.
  - How: test — "if I remove the brand, does the story still work and still make sense/be funny?" If yes, your message will be dropped.
- **Critical vs. extraneous details**: in retelling, critical details survive and extraneous ones drop out (Allport & Postman's Telephone study: ~70% of details lost in 5–6 retellings, story "sharpened" to its core). Put your message among the *critical* details.

## Key Concepts
- **Narrative transportation**: stories engross us (beginning/middle/end), so we don't argue with them — lowering counterarguing and boosting persuasion.
- **Proof by analogy**: a story about someone like me ("Lands' End replaced my coat free") makes me believe it'll happen to me too — harder to dispute than an ad claim.
- **Cultural learning**: stories transmit norms and practical knowledge faster than trial-and-error, observation, or (distrusted) ads.

## Mental Models
- Think of a story as having a **surface plot** (grabs attention) and **hidden cargo** (the lesson/brand). Design both.
- Ask the **valuable-virality test**: is the brand a *critical* detail or a *removable* one?
- **People retell, and retelling strips detail** — so anchor your message to the unforgettable core, not the trimmings.

## Anti-patterns
- **Virality without value** ("the fool in the pool"): Ron Bensimhon crashed the 2004 Olympics in a GoldenPalace.com tutu — huge buzz, but people talked about the stunt, not the casino. "Like building a magnificent Trojan Horse but forgetting to put anything inside."
- **Off-brand spectacle**: Evian "Roller Babies" — 50M+ views, *most-viewed ad ever*, yet Evian lost ~25% sales. Cute babies have nothing to do with water.
- **Forgetting it'll be retold**: if the brand isn't essential, it's exactly the detail that drops out in the retelling.

## Worked Example
**"Never Say No to Panda" (valuable virality done right).** Egyptian dairy Panda's ads: someone declines Panda cheese, a man in a panda suit silently appears and destroys the place (flips the grocery cart, kicks the food). Hilarious — but crucially, **the brand is integral**: you literally cannot tell the story without mentioning the panda, so listeners remember it's for *Panda cheese* weeks later. Contrast with Bensimhon: the casino was removable, so it vanished from the retelling.

**Dove "Evolution."** Tim Piper's clip (made with Dove, ~$100K) shows an ordinary woman transformed by makeup and Photoshop into a billboard supermodel — "No wonder our perception of beauty is distorted." It latched onto a conversation people already wanted to have (unrealistic beauty norms), made it Public, evoked Emotion, and carried Dove inside: 16M+ views, hundreds of millions in exposure, double-digit sales growth. The emotional story was the vessel; the brand rode along.

## Key Takeaways
1. People share **stories, not information** — build a Trojan Horse.
2. Aim for **valuable virality**: the brand must be **integral** to the narrative.
3. Run the test: **remove the brand — does the story still work?** If yes, fix it.
4. Stories persuade by **transportation and proof-by-analogy** — they lower counterarguing.
5. Plan for **retelling**: anchor your message to the critical, unforgettable core.
