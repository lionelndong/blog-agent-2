# Epilogue

## Core Idea
The six STEPPS work together and reinforce each other. Contagion is driven by the *content* (the product/idea and how it's framed), not by a special few "influentials" — and any product, idea, or behavior can be made contagious.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **STEPPS as an integrated system**: in real cases multiple principles compound; you rarely need all six, but combinations are powerful.
- **The forest-fire model of social epidemics (Duncan Watts)**: big fires aren't caused by big sparks — countless ordinary trees must catch and carry the flame. Likewise, contagion needs thousands of "regular Joes and Janes," not a few influentials.

## Key Concepts
- **The spark vs. the spread**: a hero may start things (Tippi Hedren; George Wright at Blendtec), but the spread depends on many ordinary sharers.
- **Designed-in vs. messaged-in STEPPS**: principles can live in the product itself (the $100 cheesesteak engineered for Social Currency) *or* in the messaging around it (Kit Kat linking to coffee for Triggers).
- **Universality**: contagion isn't luck or mystery — the same principles drive epidemics across products, services, nonprofits, health behaviors, and whole industries.

## Mental Models
- Stop asking "who can spread this?" and ask "**what about this is worth spreading?**"
- Audit any product/idea against the **six STEPPS checklist** (see cheatsheet) — even one or two well-applied principles can ignite spread.
- Contagion is **a skill, not a gift**: no big budget, marketing genius, or "creativity gene" required.

## Anti-patterns
- **Crediting only the hero/influential**: misreads how epidemics actually scale (forest fires don't depend on the exceptional spark).
- **Treating virality as random luck**: ignores the repeatable principles behind it.

## Worked Example
**Vietnamese nail salons.** In 1975, actress Tippi Hedren mentored ~20 Vietnamese refugee women at Hope Village near Sacramento; they admired her manicured nails, so she brought her manicurist and got them beauty-school training and jobs. Their success stories spread by word of mouth to other immigrants. Today ~80% of California manicurists (and 40%+ nationwide) are Vietnamese American. The pattern recurs across immigrant niches (Cambodian doughnut shops in LA; Korean dry cleaners in NYC) — and runs on all six STEPPS: job talk is **Triggered**, others' jobs are **Public**, established immigrants want to look good (**Social Currency**) and help (**Practical Value**) by telling exciting (**Emotion**) **Stories** of success. Hedren was the spark; thousands of ordinary sharers were the fire.

## Key Takeaways
1. **STEPPS compound** — real contagion usually combines several principles.
2. Epidemics are driven by **the message/content**, not a few influentials (forest-fire model).
3. Build STEPPS into the **product itself or the messaging** around it.
4. **Anything** can be contagious; it's a repeatable skill, not luck or talent.
5. When stuck, run your idea through the **six-STEPPS checklist**.
