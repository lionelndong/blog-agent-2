# Chapter 1: Social Currency

## Core Idea
People share things that make them look good. To get people talking, give them a way to mint *social currency* — to seem smart, cool, or in-the-know by talking about your product or idea.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Social Currency**: just as people spend money to buy goods, they "spend" talk to craft desired impressions. We share what makes us look good (smart, hip, in-the-know).
- **Three ways to mint social currency**:
  1. **Find inner remarkability** — surface what's novel, surprising, extreme, or pattern-breaking about the thing. *Remarkable = worthy of remark.*
     - How: ask what the thing does that no one thought possible; break an expected pattern; use mystery/controversy.
  2. **Leverage game mechanics** — rules, feedback, and levels that quantify performance and let people show status.
     - How: gamify with visible metrics (miles, badges, tiers); make status easy to understand (Olympic gold > Foursquare "School Night badge"); help people publicize achievements.
  3. **Make people feel like insiders** — use **scarcity** (limited quantity) and **exclusivity** (limited access, incl. by knowledge) so having/knowing the thing feels special.
     - How: limit availability, invite-only access, "discovery brand"; then optionally relax restrictions to build demand.

## Key Concepts
- **Inner remarkability**: the latent novelty/surprise in any product — even a toaster or black toilet paper.
- **Game mechanics**: elements (rules, feedback loops, levels) that make activity compelling and status-comparable.
- **Social comparison**: people care about relative standing, not just absolutes (the Harvard $50K-vs-$100K study: most chose earning more than peers over more absolute money).
- **Scarcity**: limited *amount* available (Please Don't Tell's 45 seats; Rue La La's 24-hour sales).
- **Exclusivity**: limited *access* — by money, celebrity, *or knowledge* (knowing a secret bar exists).
- **Intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation**: paying people to share *crowds out* the free, genuine sharing they'd do anyway.

## Mental Models
- Think of remarkable info as currency: telling a great Snapple fact makes *you* seem remarkable, so you spend it.
- Use **scarcity/exclusivity** when you want desire to rise: "if it's hard to get, it must be worth it."
- Prefer **social incentives over monetary ones** — they're cheaper and sustainable (Foursquare/airlines never pay users to talk).

## Anti-patterns
- **Paying for word of mouth**: monetary incentives crowd out intrinsic motivation; buzz becomes proportional to the payout, not the love.
- **Unintelligible status tiers**: gold/platinum/sapphire/diamond cards confuse — people can't tell where they stand, so the status loses meaning.
- **Making access feel snooty**: too many flat "no"s drive people away. Use "no, but…" to manage disappointment while keeping allure.

## Worked Example
**Rue La La vs. SmartBargains.** Ben Fischman's SmartBargains.com was a floundering discount site. He noticed its invite-only "private shopping area" was growing fast, and learned of the French *vente privée* (private sale). He launched **Rue La La**: the *same products*, but with invite-only membership and 24-hour "flash sales" that sold out by 11:03 a.m. Scarcity + exclusivity made members feel like insiders and turned them into evangelists ("when a friend tells you you've gotta try Rue La La, you believe them"). He sold both sites for $350M. Lesson: the merchandise didn't change — the *insider feeling* did.

**The McRib.** McDonald's couldn't sell the McRib on a permanent national menu. By making it *scarce* — limited-time, rotating cities — demand exploded: Facebook "bring back the McRib!" groups, fan locators, Twitter love. Same product, same price, same ingredients; only availability changed.

## Key Takeaways
1. People share to look good — so make talking about you a way to **look good**.
2. Find the **inner remarkability** in anything; remarkable = worthy of remark.
3. **Gamify** with clear, comparable status people want to show off.
4. Use **scarcity and exclusivity** to make people feel like insiders.
5. Don't pay for buzz — **social currency beats cash** and lasts longer.
