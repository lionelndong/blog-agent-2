# Patterns — Contagious Techniques

Concrete techniques from the book, organized by STEPP.

## Find the Inner Remarkability
**When to use**: a product/idea seems "boring" or undifferentiated.
**How**: surface what's novel, surprising, extreme, or pattern-breaking; break an expectation (JetBlue frills, $100 cheesesteak, black toilet paper); use mystery/controversy (Blair Witch); add surprising trivia (Snapple "Real Facts").
**Trade-offs**: remarkability drives *immediate* WOM but fades without triggers.

## Leverage Game Mechanics
**When to use**: you want sustained engagement and visible status.
**How**: quantify performance (gamify with miles/points/badges/tiers); make status instantly legible (Olympic medals beat opaque badges); help people publicize wins (Foursquare→Facebook); run contests/awards/voting that make participants market for you (Burberry "Art of the Trench").
**Trade-offs**: confusing tiers (gold/sapphire/diamond) kill the status signal.

## Make People Feel Like Insiders
**When to use**: you want desire and exclusivity-driven sharing.
**How**: limit quantity (**scarcity**) and access (**exclusivity**, incl. by knowledge/invitation); be a "discovery brand" (Please Don't Tell, Rue La La, McRib); relax restrictions later to scale.
**Trade-offs**: overdoing "no" feels snooty — use "no, but…".

## Use Social, Not Monetary, Incentives
**When to use**: motivating referrals/sharing.
**How**: harness desire to look good rather than cash; let pride/status do the work (fantasy football, frequent-flier status).
**Trade-offs**: paying crowds out intrinsic motivation; buzz becomes proportional to payout.

## Consider the Context (Trigger Design)
**When to use**: you need *ongoing* talk, not just a launch spike.
**How**: link your product to **frequent** environmental cues; place triggers **near** the desired behavior in time/place ("Man Drinks Fat" at point of soda); prefer a strong trigger over a clever-but-rarely-cued slogan.
**Trade-offs**: cues tied to many things are weak (frequency vs. strength).

## Grow the Habitat
**When to use**: few natural cues remind people of you.
**How**: repeatedly pair your product with a prevalent stimulus (Kit Kat↔coffee; Boston Market↔dinner; Michelob↔weekends).
**Trade-offs**: pick a *frequent* link (coffee), not a clever-but-rare one (cantaloupe).

## Poison Parasite
**When to use**: out-resourced by a rival.
**How**: make the rival's own message a trigger for yours (antismoking spoof of Marlboro).

## Focus on Feelings + High-Arousal Emotion
**When to use**: information alone isn't changing behavior.
**How**: evoke awe, excitement, humor, anger, anxiety, or disgust (NOT sadness/contentment); use the **Three Whys** to find the emotional core; "make them mad, not sad"; catch people when already aroused (gym, exciting shows).
**Trade-offs**: negative emotion must stay on-brand or it taints; monitor high-arousal complaints early.

## Make the Private Public
**When to use**: choices/behaviors are normally invisible.
**How**: create public signals (Movember moustaches, "I Voted" stickers, coining "ED"); design products that **advertise themselves** (Hotmail signature, "Sent from my iPhone," white earbuds, Louboutin red soles).
**Trade-offs**: for *undesirable* behaviors, do the opposite (make public private) and stress the positive norm.

## Create Behavioral Residue
**When to use**: usage is brief; you want lasting social proof.
**How**: leave durable, visible traces (Livestrong yellow wristbands; reusable Lululemon bags; trophies; Facebook Likes; reused luxury shopping bags).
**Trade-offs**: residue must be *visible* (gym bag beats private makeup case).

## Highlight Incredible Value (Deals)
**When to use**: framing a promotion to be shared.
**How**: set a high **reference point**; exploit **diminishing sensitivity**; apply the **Rule of 100** (% off under $100, $ off over $100); restrict by time/quantity/access ("limit 3 per customer").
**Trade-offs**: always-on sales reset the reference point and stop feeling like deals.

## Package Practical Value
**When to use**: you have useful expertise to spread.
**How**: bundle into short, scannable, list-style chunks (Vanguard *MoneyWhys*; "Five ways to…"); make savings/value **visible**; target **narrow** audiences (narrow content triggers a specific share).
**Trade-offs**: verify before sharing — false "useful" info spreads just as fast.

## Build a Trojan Horse (Stories)
**When to use**: you need people to carry your message naturally.
**How**: embed your product/idea in a remarkable/emotional narrative people want to tell (Dove "Evolution," Subway's Jared, Lands' End coat).
**Trade-offs**: a story lowers counterarguing and persuades by proof-by-analogy.

## Engineer Valuable Virality
**When to use**: any time you craft shareable content.
**How**: make the brand/benefit **integral** — so the story can't be told without it (Panda cheese, *Will It Blend?*); run the removal test; anchor the message to the *critical* details that survive retelling.
**Trade-offs**: virality without integration wastes the spread ("fool in the pool"; Evian roller babies).
