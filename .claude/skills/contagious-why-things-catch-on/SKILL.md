## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### STEPPS — the six principles of contagiousness
The book's spine. Six ingredients that make products, ideas, and behaviors spread by word of mouth. You don't need all six — treat them like salad toppings, not a strict recipe.

| | Principle | Mantra | Core move |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S** | Social Currency | *We share things that make us look good* | Make talking about it make people look good |
| **T** | Triggers | *Top of mind, tip of tongue* | Link it to frequent environmental cues |
| **E** | Emotion | *When we care, we share* | Evoke high-arousal emotion |
| **P** | Public | *Built to show, built to grow* | Make it observable; leave residue |
| **P** | Practical Value | *News you can use* | Package genuinely useful info |
| **S** | Stories | *Info travels under idle chatter* | Wrap it in a Trojan-Horse narrative |

### Foundational theses
- **Virality is made, not born.** Any product/idea — even a "boring" blender — can be engineered to be contagious (*Will It Blend?*).
- **Message over messenger.** Focus on what makes the *idea* worth sharing, not on finding a few "influentials." Social epidemics are forest fires carried by thousands of ordinary people, not big sparks (Duncan Watts).
- **~7% of word of mouth is online.** Offline talk is more prevalent and often more impactful. Social media is a *technology*, not a *strategy*.

### Social Currency (Ch 1)
We share to look good. Three ways to mint it: **(1) inner remarkability** (find what's novel/surprising/pattern-breaking); **(2) game mechanics** (quantify performance, give legible, show-off-able status — miles, badges, tiers); **(3) make people feel like insiders** via **scarcity** (limited amount) and **exclusivity** (limited access, incl. by knowledge). Use **social, not monetary, incentives** — paying crowds out intrinsic sharing.

### Triggers (Ch 2)
Cues that make a product top of mind → tip of tongue. Interest drives *immediate* WOM; **triggers drive ongoing WOM**. **Consider the context**: pick **frequent** cues placed **near the desired behavior** (Kit Kat↔coffee; "Man Drinks Fat" at point of soda). Frequency must be balanced with **strength** — a cue tied to everything triggers nothing. **Grow the habitat** by pairing your product with a prevalent stimulus.

### Emotion (Ch 3)
**Arousal, not positivity, drives sharing.** High-arousal emotions share (awe, excitement, humor, anger, anxiety, disgust); low-arousal ones suppress (sadness, contentment). **Focus on feelings, not features**; use the **Three Whys** to find the emotional core; **make them mad, not sad**. Even physical arousal (running in place) boosts sharing.

### Public (Ch 4)
"Monkey see, monkey do" — people imitate only what they can **see**. Use **social proof** (a line means good food). **Make the private public** (Movember moustaches; "I Voted" stickers), design products that **advertise themselves** (white iPod earbuds; "Sent from my iPhone"), and create **behavioral residue** — durable visible traces (Livestrong wristbands). To *stop* a behavior, do the inverse: **make the public private** and stress the positive norm (anti-drug ads backfire by signaling "everyone does it").

### Practical Value (Ch 5)
**News you can use** — people share useful info to help others. Value is judged relative to a **reference point** (prospect theory), with **diminishing sensitivity**. Use the **Rule of 100**: % off under $100, $ off over $100. **Restrict availability** (time/quantity/access) to raise perceived value. **Package** expertise into short, scannable units; target **narrow** audiences; make value **visible**. Verify before sharing — false "useful" info spreads fast.

### Stories (Ch 6)
People think in narratives. Build a **Trojan Horse** — a story people want to tell that carries your message inside. Aim for **valuable virality**: make the brand **integral**, so the story can't be told without it (Panda cheese; *Will It Blend?*). Virality without integration wastes the spread ("fool in the pool"; Evian roller babies). Stories persuade via transportation and **proof by analogy**; plan for retelling (critical details survive, extraneous ones drop out).
